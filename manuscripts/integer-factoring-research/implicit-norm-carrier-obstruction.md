# Stable finite algebra carriers for the quadratic phase

**Scope:** retained separate research branch; not part of the selected conditional theorem review. See the [curated dependency and review map](../../problems/integer-factorization/sources.md).

Status: elementary structural obstruction, not an arithmetic lower bound.

Let R=Z/MZ and let c be a unit of R. Suppose S is a commutative unital
R-algebra generated by at most d elements as an R-module. Suppose

```text
  sigma:S->S is a unital R-algebra endomorphism,
  sigma(u)=c u for an element u of S,
  phi:S->R is a unital R-algebra homomorphism, and
  a=phi(u) is a unit in R.
```


Then, for every prime p dividing M,

```text
  ord_p(c) <= d.
```


Neither S being free nor u being a unit inside S is required. Sigma
need not be an automorphism. The unit condition is only on the scalar
evaluation phi(u).

Proof. Reduce everything modulo p. The F_p-algebra S_p=S/pS has
dimension e<=d; it is nonzero because phi descends to a unital map
S_p->F_p. Let chi(X) be the characteristic polynomial of multiplication
by u on S_p. Cayley-Hamilton gives chi(u)=0 in S_p. For every j>=0,
apply phi composed with sigma^j to this identity. As sigma fixes the
coefficient field and sigma^j(u)=c^j u, we obtain

```text
  chi(a c^j)=0 in F_p.
```


Because a and c are nonzero, these values include ord_p(c) distinct
roots of a monic polynomial of degree e. Therefore ord_p(c)<=e<=d.

A useful certification form. Set

```text
  Q_d(c)=prod_(k=1)^d (1-c^k) in R.
```


If gcd(Q_d(c),M)=1, no such algebra carrier of module rank/generator
bound d can exist. This is exactly the certificate that ord_p(c)>d
for every prime factor p of M. Q_d(c) can be evaluated by the known
degree-one geometric q-factorial method in soft-O(sqrt(d)) operations,
plus exponent initialization and a gcd. This verification does not
require the factorization of M or a known order. A nonunit result
instead enters the existing factor/short-period handling branch.

Free automorphism/unit special case. If S is free of rank d, sigma is
an automorphism, and u is a unit in S, then one gets the stronger
whole-modulus relation c^d=1 directly. The multiplication operators
m_u and m_(sigma(u)) are conjugate by sigma, while m_(sigma(u))=c m_u.
Their equal determinants imply (c^d-1)det(m_u)=0. The determinant is a
unit, so c^d=1 in R.

Application to the quadratic-phase product. With v=q^A, define the
endomorphism of the Laurent polynomial ring by

```text
  sigma(U)=v^2 U,
  sigma(Z)=v U Z.
```


Then sigma^t(Z)=Z U^t v^(t^2). The product in question is the orbit
norm of f_H(Z)=prod_(i<H)(1-q^(-i)Z), evaluated at Z=q^C,U=q^B.
A finite algebra intended to preserve this sigma-action and support
the scalar evaluation has c=v^2=q^(2A) and phi(U)=q^B, a unit. Hence
any exact stable carrier with at most d module generators forces all
local curvature orders ord_p(q^(2A)) to be <=d. In the certified high
local curvature-order branch, the usual finite-algebra automorphism-
norm/modular-composition shortcut therefore cannot be applied in this
form.

Examples of why apparent finite quotients fail. The quotient by U^d
is stable under U->cU, but it admits no evaluation U->q^B with q^B a
unit. A cyclic quotient U^d-b with b a unit can remain stable only if
c^d=1. More generally, allowing a noninvertible endomorphism does not
avoid the theorem if the unit scalar evaluation is retained.

Scope. This does not exclude moving moduli with explicit boundary
corrections, quotients whose relevant dimension grows beyond the local
order, a representation whose final extraction is not an algebra
homomorphism, or another implicit norm/resultant algorithm that does
not preserve the full scaling endomorphism inside one finite algebra.
It is not a generic lower bound for the desired scalar product.
