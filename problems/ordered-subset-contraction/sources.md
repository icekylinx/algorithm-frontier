# Sources, scope and attribution

The curated derivations and verification adapters are attributed to **icekylinx and GPT-6 Astra Pro**, under the repository's Apache-2.0 license. This directory selectively imports completed results from the supplied SAT checkpoint; it excludes SAT claims, proposed square-permanent routes, failed attempts and review archives. Historical priority of the precise general subset aggregation is unresolved.

The source fragments used are `paper/subset_zeta_triple_extension.tex`, `paper/integer_subset_capacity_extension.tex`, `paper/weighted_three_block_capacity.tex`, `paper/balanced_three_block_extension.tex`, `paper/growing_degree_balanced_extension.tex`, `paper/quadratic_character_support.tex`, and the cyclic circuit construction. The uniform Bell theorem is not imported as a competing growing-degree result; only its useful fixed-degree algorithm survives. Unequal-domain position-word and nested-domain variants are omitted because the general arbitrary weighted subset theorem is the selected entry's substantive scope.

Primary mathematical sources:

- Volker Strassen, *Gaussian Elimination Is Not Optimal*, Numerische Mathematik 13 (1969), 354–356, [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02165411). Its explicit seven-product integer bilinear identity is the established universal-ring fallback. This does not require the recent sources below.
- Andreas Björklund, Petteri Kaski and Łukasz Kowalik, *Counting Thin Subgraphs via Packings Faster than Meet-in-the-Middle Time*, [author preprint, arXiv:1306.4111](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.4111), Theorems 1–2. This is the direct prior weighted-disjoint-triples problem. Its theorem fixes the subset size independently of universe size, with integer weights and exact division permitted; Theorem 2 specializes to sizes two, three and four. Section 1.4 discusses a uniform binomial-sensitive extension as an open direction. The square branch's exact exponents for those small sizes agree with their stated pattern. The selected theorem explicitly controls growing sizes and preserves division-free factor order. Its unspecified exponential prefactor does not settle that source's literal all-range binomial-tail question. No superiority or historical novelty over all later work is asserted.
- V. Arvind, Abhranil Chatterjee, Rajit Datta and Partha Mukhopadhyay, *On Explicit Branching Programs for the Rectangular Determinant and Permanent Polynomials*, ISAAC 2019, Theorem 2, [primary proceedings](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ISAAC.2019.38). The explicit branching-program comparison is the binomial sum through `floor(k/2)`. The present constructions allow general arithmetic circuits; branching-program lower bounds do not apply to them.
- Andreas Björklund, Thore Husfeldt, Petteri Kaski and Mikko Koivisto, *Evaluation of Permanents in Rings and Semirings*, IPL 110 (2010), 867–870, [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.3251). Prior arbitrary-ring rectangular evaluation context.
- Gian-Carlo Rota, Bruce Sagan and Paul R. Stein, *A Cyclic Derivative in Noncommutative Algebra*, Journal of Algebra 64 (1980), 54–75, [author-hosted paper](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/bsagan/Papers/Old/cdn-pub.pdf). The cyclic derivative itself is prior work. The imported implementation is a shared-circuit adapter and row-cut construction, with its own exact finite checks.

Additional recent dependencies, used only in their verified published coefficient models:

- Yang Zhou and Mingsheng Wang, *Shared-leg Rectangular Matrix Multiplication over Arbitrary Rings*, [source repository](https://github.com/Hugin-Hilbert/rectangular-matmul-rings), source checkpoint pin `285f513384dee8f933d941ed5e8770ddfbdcaae2` (14 October 2026). The public square exponent `9/4` supplies the second universal ring branch through square tiling. The public repository covers arbitrary associative rings, including nonunital rings. The import uses no stronger arbitrary-three-shape universal interface, and does not claim to rerun the public Lean endpoint.
- Alimzhan Amanov, Maxim van den Berg and Jeroen Zuiddam, *Multiplying Matrices via Polynomials: Exposition and Barriers*, [arXiv:2610.30054v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30054v1), **Theorem 1.2** supplies the full rectangular curve over **every field**. Section 4.5, equation (4.4), Lemma 4.15, and Sections 4.5–4.6 supply the quadratic relaxation, its exact support and weighted profile argument. The full rectangular `U` branch, its fixed odd-degree alternatives and its characteristic-two improvement here are restricted to scalar field coefficients. Fix the field first; no universal integer family or field-independent prefactor is inferred.

**Independent dependency boundary.** The stronger [integral three-shape composition](../noncommutative-crossing/sources.md) is curated separately, with a local mathematical deduction and explicit integral source premises. This subset package does not require it: the universal `3/8` leading coefficient uses only the public square `9/4` endpoint and square tiling. Its full rectangular refinements continue to use the cited field theorem. No local formal artifacts are imported or certified for either composition; the public source's arbitrary-three-shape corollary is distinguished from its public Lean endpoint.
