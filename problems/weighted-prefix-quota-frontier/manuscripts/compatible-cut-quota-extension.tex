\section{Exact quotas on compatible cuts}\label{af-3beb839889-exact-quotas-on-compatible-cuts}}

The laminar algorithm also handles a family whose sets are pairwise nested, disjoint, or together cover the whole numeric ground. Such a family can describe quotas on either side of edges of an unrooted tree. Fixing the total numeric mass lets every set be oriented away from one anchor, after which the constraints are laminar.

This is a direct extension of \href{https://github.com/icekylinx/algorithm-frontier/blob/main/problems/weighted-prefix-quota-frontier/manuscripts/laminar-dominant-weight-recovery.md}{the exact laminar recovery theorem}. It preserves one arbitrary integral polymatroid resource, every original mandatory total, and the original signed scalar costs, including costs on zero-weight coordinates. The extension introduces no additional optimization theorem.

\hypertarget{af-3beb839889-scope-and-theorem}{%
\subsection{1. Scope and theorem}\label{af-3beb839889-scope-and-theorem}}

Use one common nonnegative integer weight vector \(w\) in every numeric row, root cap \(w\cdot x\le B\), and the resource and objective model of \href{https://github.com/icekylinx/algorithm-frontier/blob/main/problems/weighted-prefix-quota-frontier/proof.md\#1-model-and-theorem}{the main theorem}. Remove individually prohibited positive coordinates by the usual safe preparation, keeping the original required total. Zero-weight coordinates contribute nothing to numeric rows and keep their resource role and scalar costs. Let \(P\) be the remaining positive numeric ground.

After projecting numeric sets onto \(P\), suppose every pair \(A,C\) obeys at least one of

\[
A\subseteq C,\qquad C\subseteq A,\qquad
A\cap C=\varnothing,\qquad A\cup C=P.
\tag{CC1}
\]

This explicit condition is often called \emph{cross-free}. It is checkable in polynomial time on the listed sets. Projection preserves CC1, so a family satisfying it on the original ground also satisfies it after preparation. The root and empty sets cause no difficulty.

\textbf{Theorem.} Exact signed-scalar optimization under CC1 admits the same deterministic parameter bounds as the laminar problem:

\[
2^{\frac{10}{9}B^2+O(B\log(B+1))}\mathrm{poly}(H)
\tag{CC2}
\]

with the complete elementary coloring construction, or

\[
2^{(5/9+o(1))B^2}\mathrm{poly}(H)
\tag{CC3}
\]

using the specifically cited locally explicit universal-set theorem in \href{https://github.com/icekylinx/algorithm-frontier/blob/main/problems/weighted-prefix-quota-frontier/manuscripts/laminar-dominant-weight-recovery.md\#83-the-precise-published-construction-and-the-space-claim}{the recovery proof, Section 8.3}. Both use uniform polynomial binary workspace. For a prescribed total numeric mass, no loop over that mass is needed, and the actual-depth, actual-weight and projected-coloring bounds of the laminar theorem apply to the oriented instance.

With one positive weight value, the polynomial-time endpoint extends when the total numeric mass is prescribed or otherwise forced. For example, if every coordinate is positive with weight \(w\) and a mandatory resource base has original total \(R\), its numeric mass is forced to be \(wR\). The statement does not infer a polynomial-time endpoint merely by enumerating an unknown, binary-large mass when zero coordinates allow the positive count to vary.

\hypertarget{af-3beb839889-orienting-every-set-away-from-an-anchor}{%
\subsection{2. Orienting every set away from an anchor}\label{af-3beb839889-orienting-every-set-away-from-an-anchor}}

Assume \(P\ne\varnothing\), and choose any anchor \(p\in P\). Replace a set by its side that avoids the anchor:

\[
A^{\circ}=
\begin{cases}
A,&p\notin A,\\
P\setminus A,&p\in A.
\end{cases}
\tag{CC4}
\]

The distinct nonempty sets \(A^{\circ}\) form a laminar family.

If both original sets avoid \(p\), their union cannot be \(P\), so CC1 already says they are nested or disjoint. If both contain \(p\), they cannot be disjoint; nested sets have reversely nested complements, and sets with union \(P\) have disjoint complements. Finally suppose \(p\in A\) and \(p\notin C\). The possible relations in CC1 give:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  If \(C\subseteq A\), then \(C\) and \(P\setminus A\) are disjoint.
\item
  If \(A\cap C=\varnothing\), then \(C\subseteq P\setminus A\).
\item
  If \(A\cup C=P\), then \(P\setminus A\subseteq C\).
\end{itemize}

The remaining inclusion \(A\subseteq C\) is impossible because of \(p\). This proves laminarity in every case. Adding the full set \(P\) as a root keeps it laminar.

\hypertarget{af-3beb839889-exact-numeric-correspondence-at-fixed-total-mass}{%
\subsection{3. Exact numeric correspondence at fixed total mass}\label{af-3beb839889-exact-numeric-correspondence-at-fixed-total-mass}}

Fix an integer \(M\in[0,B]\) and impose the exact root equality

\[
\sum_{e\in P}w_e x_e=M.
\tag{CC5}
\]

A row \(\ell_A\le\sum_{e\in A}w_e x_e\le u_A\) is unchanged when \(p\notin A\). When \(p\in A\), replace it by

\[
M-u_A\le\sum_{e\in P\setminus A}w_e x_e\le M-\ell_A.
\tag{CC6}
\]

Under CC5, CC6 is exactly the original row. An absent upper bound gives no lower restriction on the complement; an absent lower bound is zero. Intersect every new interval with \([0,M]\), reject an empty interval, and reject a positive lower requirement on an empty set. Merge repeated oriented sets by taking the largest lower and smallest upper bound. Unlike merely merging auxiliary coloring identities, this merge is valid for the numeric constraints themselves because the fixed total makes their rewritten left sides identical.

The oriented rows together with CC5 form a common-weight laminar instance with root cap \(M\le B\). The shared resource and its required total have not been modified. Neither the scalar coefficients nor the zero-coordinate ground changes. Thus the feasible original allocations of mass \(M\) are exactly the feasible allocations of this transformed instance, with exactly the same objective values. Apply the laminar algorithm and keep its original-coordinate output.

The common weight vector is essential: one total \(M\) would not give CC6 for unrelated row-specific weight vectors. No such extension is asserted.

\hypertarget{af-3beb839889-unknown-total-binary-input-and-workspace}{%
\subsection{4. Unknown total, binary input and workspace}\label{af-3beb839889-unknown-total-binary-input-and-workspace}}

If \(P\) is empty, all numeric row values are zero. Check their lower bounds and optimize the original signed scalar on the remaining zero-weight resource at the original required total. This is the existing polynomial zero-only case.

Otherwise let \(n=|P|\). If \(B<n\), enumerate \(M=0,\ldots,B\), perform Sections 2--3, and keep the best original feasible answer. Every feasible allocation belongs to exactly one of these total-mass cases. The loop has at most \(B+1\) iterations, and all its laminar calls have parameter at most \(B\). Its additional logarithmic time factor is absorbed in CC2--CC3. Store only the current mass, one transformed instance and the best binary output. The positive copy ground used inside each laminar call is polynomial because \(B<n\).

If \(B\ge n\), use the existing direct count-vector fallback on the original positive coordinates. There are at most \((B+1)^n\) vectors with \(0\le x_e\le\lfloor B/w_e\rfloor\). Test \textbf{every original numeric row}, test the positive resource vector, and perform the exact compressed zero completion with its signed costs. This method does not require laminar geometry and takes \(2^{O(B\log(B+1))}\mathrm{poly}(H)\) time. It stores \(n\) binary counters, not an array indexed by numerical \(B\).

If a mass \(M\) is prescribed, process just that mass. In the one-positive- weight case, reject a mass not divisible by the positive weight, orient the rows, and use the direct homogeneous completion on original coordinates. The binary count-rank oracle avoids unit expansion even for very large resource totals and bounds. This proves the polynomial endpoint in the stated fixed-mass scope.

\hypertarget{af-3beb839889-interpretation-as-compatible-tree-cuts}{%
\subsection{5. Interpretation as compatible tree cuts}\label{af-3beb839889-interpretation-as-compatible-tree-cuts}}

For a tree with coordinates on its vertices, removing an edge produces two complementary vertex sets. Quota rows may choose either side of each such cut, and may include both sides. Two tree-edge cuts have compatible bipartitions: among the four pairwise intersections of their sides, at least one is empty. For any chosen sides, this says exactly that they are disjoint, nested, or cover the whole ground, as in CC1.

Choosing the anchor fixes a root of the tree; CC4 orients the selected cuts away from it. The resulting subsets are rooted subtrees and hence laminar. Numeric bounds on a side containing the root are translated by the fixed total, not dropped. The same reasoning applies when some tree vertices carry no coordinates, by projection.

More generally, CC1 is the full hypothesis used by the proof; a tree representation is unnecessary. Arbitrary crossing sets or arbitrary circular intervals need not satisfy it and are not included.

\subsection{6. Independent finite evidence and dependencies}\label{af-3beb839889-independent-finite-evidence-and-dependencies}}

Its compatible-cut part enumerates all 2,629 families satisfying CC1 on grounds of at most four elements and all 10,441 anchor choices for those families. The geometric tests directly check laminarity of the oriented families. These checks isolate the new orientation and numeric correspondence; they do not reimplement the resource solver or prove the asymptotic running time.
