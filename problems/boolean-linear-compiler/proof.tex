\section{Exact Boolean optimization by interval and slack compilation}\label{af-c97649ed9d-exact-boolean-optimization-by-interval-and-slack-compilation}}

The strongest local refinement is the \href{https://github.com/icekylinx/algorithm-frontier/blob/main/problems/boolean-linear-compiler/manuscripts/cardinality-and-modal-interval-compilation.md}{cardinality and coefficient-normalized compiler}. For each nonconstant row, subtract a most frequent coefficient on each exact cardinality layer. If \(d_i\) is the resulting support, put \(\bar\kappa_i=\min\{W_i+1,d_i,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}\) and \(\bar Q=\sum_i\lceil\log_2\bar\kappa_i\rceil\). For \(0\le B\le\bar Q\), its time is

\[
\mathrm{poly}(L)P_{\rm card}
2^{\alpha n+\bar Q-(1-\alpha)B},
\]

with the same backend space bound at \(n+B\) variables. Here \(P_{\rm card}\) is the maximum of one and the actual catalogue product over nontrivial cardinality layers. Original variables and rows are explicit, so their counts are at most \(L\). The original support-sized argument below remains an independent unconditioned lemma.

\hypertarget{af-c97649ed9d-unconditioned-compiler-theorem}{%
\subsection{1. Unconditioned compiler theorem}\label{af-c97649ed9d-unconditioned-compiler-theorem}}

The input has \(n\) Boolean variables, signed binary integer equalities, and interval rows \(\ell_i\le a_i^\top x\le u_i\). Let \(L\) be its binary length, \(s_i=|\mathrm{supp}(a_i)|\), and \(W_i=u_i-\ell_i\). Normalize one-sided rows against their attainable ranges and handle empty intervals and zero rows directly. Put \[
\kappa_i=\min\{W_i+1,s_i\},\quad q_i=\lceil\log_2\kappa_i\rceil,\quad Q=\sum_iq_i.
\] Assume a deterministic Subset Sum backend returns indexed witnesses in time \(\mathrm{poly}(L')2^{\alpha N}\), and, if supplied, writable space \(\mathrm{poly}(L')2^{\sigma N}\). For any integer \(0\le B\le Q\), the compiler gives \[
T_B\le\mathrm{poly}(L)\left(\prod_i p_i\right)
2^{\alpha n+Q-(1-\alpha)B},\qquad
S_B\le\mathrm{poly}(L)2^{\sigma(n+B)},
\] where \(p_i\le2\lceil\log_2(W_i+1)\rceil+1\), and \(p_i=1\) if \(W_i+1\le s_i\). Exact equalities contribute no slack parameter. Feasibility, indexed witnesses, linear optimization, and explicitly listed polynomial-size lexicographic optimization are supported.

\hypertarget{af-c97649ed9d-signed-interval-lemma}{%
\subsection{2. Signed interval lemma}\label{af-c97649ed9d-signed-interval-lemma}}

For a nonzero row with original support \(s\ge1\), stop when the current interval contains at most \(s\) integers. Otherwise let \(t\) be the number of odd current coefficients, set \(a'_j=\lfloor a_j/2\rfloor\), and put

\[
\ell'=\lceil\ell/2\rceil,\qquad u'=\lfloor(u-t)/2\rfloor.
\]

Emit the nonempty intervals

\[
[\ell,\min\{u,2\ell'+t-2\}],\qquad
[\max\{\ell,2u'+2\},u]
\]

using the current coefficient row. Recurse once with \(a'\) on \([\ell',u']\) if it is nonempty. For every Boolean assignment, including for signed coefficients,

\[
a^\top x=2(a')^\top x+\delta(x),\qquad
\delta(x)=\sum_{j:a_j\text{ odd}}x_j\in[0,t].
\]

Thus a recursive acceptance has original value between \(2\ell'\ge\ell\) and \(2u'+t\le u\). Conversely, if a parent-feasible assignment has rounded sum below \(\ell'\), its original sum is at most \(2\ell'+t-2\) and falls in the lower boundary. A rounded sum above \(u'\) has original sum at least \(2u'+2\) and falls in the upper boundary. When \(\ell'>u'\), these two cases cover every assignment and no recursion is needed. Clipping keeps boundary acceptances inside the parent interval.

The lower boundary has at most \(2\ell'-\ell+t-1\le t\) values; the upper has at most \(u-2u'-1\le t\) values. When \(t=0\), both are empty. Every emitted interval therefore contains at most \(s\) values, or at most \(W+1\) if the initial row is already short. A recursive width is at most half its parent's width, so at most two emissions per halving and one terminal emission prove \(p_i\le2\lceil\log_2(W_i+1)\rceil+1\). The depth and all intermediate bit lengths are polynomial in the binary input size. A zero row is checked directly and removed before defining \(q_i\); a reversed interval is infeasible.

This proves exact disjunction for each original Boolean assignment. Boundary overlaps are harmless: they are counted among the catalogue's actual tests, and the construction has only one recursive child. The \href{https://github.com/icekylinx/algorithm-frontier/blob/main/problems/boolean-linear-compiler/manuscripts/boolean-linear-optimization-compiler.md\#2-signed-short-interval-catalogue}{complete proof} treats the empty-child and negative-floor cases explicitly. The \href{https://github.com/icekylinx/algorithm-frontier/blob/main/problems/boolean-linear-compiler/verify/check_interval_compiler.py}{independent checker} also compares full compiled solution sets with the original systems.

\hypertarget{af-c97649ed9d-slack-and-branch-count}{%
\subsection{3. Slack and branch count}\label{af-c97649ed9d-slack-and-branch-count}}

Choose \(0\le b_i\le q_i\) summing to \(B\). Split each catalogue interval into chunks of at most \(2^{b_i}\) values. There are at most \(2^{q_i-b_i}\) chunks, so the number of branch tuples is at most \[
\left(\prod_i p_i\right)2^{Q-B}.
\] For a positive-width chunk \([l,u]\), put \(w=u-l\), \(q=\lceil\log_2(w+1)\rceil\le b_i\). The \(q\) weights \[
1,2,\ldots,2^{q-2},\quad w-(2^{q-1}-1)
\] have subset sums exactly \(0,\ldots,w\). For \(q=1\), the initial list is empty. The powers cover the initial interval; the final shift overlaps or abuts it and ends at \(w\). Thus the chunk becomes one equality with at most \(b_i\) Boolean slack variables. Different rows use different variables; width zero needs none. This is a witness reduction, not a parsimonious counting reduction.

\hypertarget{af-c97649ed9d-signed-radix-packing}{%
\subsection{4. Signed radix packing}\label{af-c97649ed9d-signed-radix-packing}}

For a branch with equations \(c_j^\top y=t_j\) on \(N\le n+B\) variables, use \[
R=1+\max_j\left(\sum_k|c_{jk}|+|t_j|\right),\qquad
A_k=\sum_jc_{jk}R^j,\qquad T=\sum_jt_jR^j.
\] Handle the all-zero system directly; otherwise \(R\ge2\). The packed equation \(\sum_kA_ky_k=T\) is equivalent to all branch equations. Indeed \(d_j=c_j^\top y-t_j\) satisfies \(|d_j|<R\). Reducing \(\sum_jd_jR^j=0\) modulo \(R\) forces \(d_0=0\); divide and repeat. This proof permits signed digits.

The packed bit length is polynomial in \(L\). Multiplying the branch bound by the backend cost at \(n+B\) proves the time formula. Branches run sequentially with one backend workspace, proving the space formula. A witness is restricted to its original \(n\) coordinates.

\hypertarget{af-c97649ed9d-optimization-and-backend-conditions}{%
\subsection{5. Optimization and backend conditions}\label{af-c97649ed9d-optimization-and-backend-conditions}}

Binary search over the polynomial-bit objective range uses polynomially many feasibility queries. Completely enumerate the short-target catalogue for the one added threshold row; its cost is polynomial and it adds no slack variables. Fix an optimum as an exact equality before treating the next lexicographic objective.

For a fixed number of interval rows, \(Q=O(\log n)\) and \(\prod_i p_i\) is polynomial in \(L\), so the backend's separate time and space interfaces transfer up to polynomial factors. A time-only backend supplies no new simultaneous space bound. Numerical runtimes use the full theorem and indexed-witness scope of the chosen backend; scalar exponent checks alone do not establish that separate theorem.

\hypertarget{af-c97649ed9d-cardinality-and-common-coefficient-refinement}{%
\subsection{6. Cardinality and common-coefficient refinement}\label{af-c97649ed9d-cardinality-and-common-coefficient-refinement}}

On the retained equality \(\sum_{j=1}^n x_j=k\), subtract a most frequent row coefficient \(c_i\) and shift both endpoints by \(c_i k\). The transformed support is \(d_i=n-\max_c|\{j:a_{ij}=c\}|\le s_i\), and the interval width is unchanged. A constant row becomes the direct test \(\ell_i\le c_i k\le u_i\), without a logarithmic parameter.

If a current row has \(t\) odd coefficients, signed floor gives a remainder in

\[
[A,D]=[\max\{0,k+t-n\},\min\{k,t\}],
\qquad D-A=\min\{k,n-k,t,n-t\}.
\]

The safe child is \([\lceil(\ell-A)/2\rceil,\lfloor(u-D)/2\rfloor]\). Its two clipped boundary intervals have at most \(D-A\) values. Recursing only on that child yields the cap \(\kappa_i(k)=\min\{W_i+1,d_i,k,n-k\}\) and the same logarithmic catalogue count. The \href{https://github.com/icekylinx/algorithm-frontier/blob/main/problems/boolean-linear-compiler/manuscripts/cardinality-and-modal-interval-compilation.md\#2-exact-coefficient-normalization-within-a-layer}{companion proof, Sections 2--4}, includes empty children, negative common coefficients, constant parity remainders, overlapping boundaries and polynomial bit lengths.

Keep the cardinality equality on original variables, with zero slack coefficients, in the signed radix system. Set \(Q_k=\sum_i\lceil\log_2\kappa_i(k)\rceil\) and \(B_k=\min\{B,Q_k\}\). Then \(Q_k-B_k\le\bar Q-B\) and \(B_k\le B\), proving the displayed uniform time/space bound even when \(B>Q_k\) or \(\alpha>1\). The layers \(k=0,n\) and \(n\le1\) are direct cases. Original equalities and objective values retain their original meaning; an objective threshold is centered only under the same guard and its short tests are enumerated with no additional slack variables.
