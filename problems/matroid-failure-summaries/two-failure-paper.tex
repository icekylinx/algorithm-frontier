\begin{abstract}
Fix an order on a rank-\(r\) matroid and assign at most one failure label to
each item. We study the unique minimum subset preserving every ordered-prefix
rank after at most two label failures. Its worst-case size is exactly \(3r\)
through rank three and exactly 13 at rank four. General upper bounds follow
from one-failure replacement profiles and a circuit missing-pair argument.
If the one-failure union adds only one new item, appearing under \(t\)
single failures, the sharp size is \(r+2t\). We give a profile-sensitive
construction and a 13-item nonrepresentable obstruction. The proofs allow
loops, unlabelled items, rank drops and arbitrary fixed signed costs.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{A direct construction.}
Let \(N\) be the number of items. Compute the canonical basis \(B_0\).
For each label on \(B_0\), compute the basis after that label fails; for
each label on each resulting basis, compute the basis after both labels
fail. Retain their union. For a target failure set, a basis avoiding all
remaining failed labels is unchanged by those deletions. Otherwise at least
one failed label occurs on that basis and is a branch of the construction.
Following at most two such branches therefore reaches the target canonical
basis. Every queried set is itself allowed, so the retained union is exactly
the union over all allowed failures. There are at most \(1+r+r^2\) scans
and at most \(N(1+r+r^2)\) independence queries after fixing the order.
The proofs below establish its size, uniqueness and improved structured case.
