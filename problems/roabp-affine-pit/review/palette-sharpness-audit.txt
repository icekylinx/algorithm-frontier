1. BASE CASE AND DOMAIN

The palette is P={0=p_0<p_1<...<p_m=W}, W>=1. If its only positive
element is W, use I+x_j E_(j,j+1) for j=1,...,W-1. Every local matrix
is unipotent, so every interval has rank W at every point. A path from
state 1 to state W must take every consecutive off-diagonal transition,
and there is exactly one such path. The (1,W) scalar is therefore
product_j x_j, over every characteristic. The factor count W-1 equals
h({0,W}). For W=1, the empty word and scalar 1 correctly realize h=0.

The theorem uses P as an allowed rank palette. It does not require every
rank in P to occur as the rank of a nonempty interval. This is consistent
with both its explicit local-factor statement and the palette upper bound.
For example, an empty top-level recursive word need not insert an unused
rank-W factor solely to realize that rank in a nonempty interval.

2. INDUCTION AND LOCAL RANKS

Let r be the least positive level and k=W-r. The shifted lower palette
Q={0,p_2-r,...,p_m-r=k} has smaller width. Inductively choose a width-k
word H with standard endpoints alpha,beta and scalar the full squarefree
variable product. Make r+1 disjoint copies H_0,...,H_r, embedding every
LOCAL factor as diag(I_r,A). These are still univariate local factors;
no multivariate product is treated as one local matrix.

For j=1,...,r, insert
  B_j=P_r+x_j E_(r+beta,j),
  C_j=P_r+y_j E_(j,r+alpha).
For B_j the added passive row is x_j times active row j; for C_j the
added passive column is y_j times active column j. Each therefore has
generic rank exactly r and constant P_r, with its top-left r-minor equal
to one. Embedded local factors have generic and constant ranks r+s,
where s is the corresponding lower-word rank. These belong to P.

All newly introduced variables and all copied variables are distinct.
The widths, endpoint indices, and ranges of j are valid even when k=1
and the lower word is empty. In that case alpha=beta=1 and H_a is the
one-by-one identity, so the bridge computation below still applies.

3. EXACT SCALAR, INCLUDING EARLIER ACTIVE COORDINATES

Start with the passive row e_(r+alpha)^T. The first embedded copy gives
passive row e_alpha^T H_0 and no active coordinates. B_1 kills the
passive part and sends its beta entry to active coordinate 1, with factor
x_1. C_1 retains that active entry and places y_1 times it at passive
coordinate alpha.

After bridge pair j, all active support is among indices 1,...,j, and
the passive row is q_j e_alpha^T. The next embedded copy leaves all
active coordinates unchanged and transforms the passive row to
q_j e_alpha^T H_j. B_(j+1) retains the earlier active entries and sends
the beta entry of that passive row into the NEW coordinate j+1. There
was no earlier contribution at j+1. C_(j+1) sends only this new active
coordinate back into the passive coordinate alpha; older active entries
cannot generate an unwanted passive excursion.

Thus exactly
  q_j=(product_{a=1}^j x_a y_a)
      (product_{a=0}^{j-1} H_a(alpha,beta)).
The final embedded copy and passive beta sink give
  (product_{j=1}^r x_j y_j)
  (product_{a=0}^r H_a(alpha,beta)).
The induction hypothesis turns this into the product of every original
variable, coefficient one. The recurrence involves no subtraction or
division, so characteristic two and all other fields are included.

4. FACTOR COUNT AND EVERY CONTIGUOUS INTERVAL

If N=h(Q), the number of variables and factors is (r+1)N+2r, equal to
  (r+1)(h(Q)+2)-2=h(P).
The successive gaps of Q are exactly the later gaps of P, so the product
formula follows without an additional term.

Every local constant is a nested flag projection P_s, and its generic
local rank is exactly s. On any nonempty interval, let s_min be the
minimum of those local values. The constant interval product is P_s_min,

rank is at most the minimum local rank, hence at most s_min. Equality
follows for every interval, regardless of its position inside or across
recursive copies and bridges. This argument also proves the upper rank
identities over the polynomial ring, not only at selected evaluations.
