1. THE MACRO IS AN ORIGINAL-FIELD READ-ONCE REALIZATION

Every base factor is P_s+zE_ab, a!=b, with PE=E or EP=E. Thus E^2=0.
The two replacements are exactly
  (P+x^2 E)(I-yE)=P+(x^2-y)E when PE=E,
  (I-yE)(P+x^2 E)=P+(x^2-y)E when EP=E.
No division or characteristic hypothesis is used. These are two distinct
original univariate factors, with degrees at most two and one. The first
rank-s factor is P times an invertible shear, or an invertible shear times
P, and therefore has rank exactly s. The other factor is unipotent and
has rank W. Their coefficients remain in the original field. The shifted
parameter t never enters an original local matrix coefficient.

Substitution into the exact base scalar identity gives
  f=product_e(x_e^2-y_e)
as a polynomial identity. All variables are distinct; the macro notation
does not hide a multivariate local factor or a repeated variable.

2. ALL PHYSICAL INTERVALS ARE CHECKED

At any coefficient-field point with C_y=C_x^2, put U=I+C_x^2E. Since
U^(-1)=I-C_x^2E, the constant macro is respectively
  P U, U^(-1)       or       U^(-1), U P.
Every complete macro is P. In an arbitrary contiguous interval, the
interior macros are complete. Only its two boundary macros may be partial,
and the displayed forms put their surviving shear on the outside of the
projector product. If the interval lies inside one macro, the same claim
is checked directly for each single factor or the complete pair.

Thus the evaluated interval is an invertible left factor, followed by a
product of nested projections, followed by an invertible right factor.
Its rank equals the minimum local rank actually present (or W if there
are only invertible factors). Its generic rank is at most that same
minimum, and at least the evaluated rank. Equality proves preservation.

This does not commute a shear through a projector. It also does not assume
that only macro-aligned intervals matter. Constant identity padding can
be inserted anywhere, including between the two local factors of a macro,
without changing the cancellation or partial-boundary analysis.

3. THE ACTUAL MOMENT CURVE AND ALL CHARACTERISTICS

For N>=1, x_e uses observed coordinate 2e-1 and y_e uses coordinate 4e-2.
The former indices are odd and distinct, the latter even and distinct,
so all 2N coordinates are different and their maximum is 4N-2. Padding
to n=4N-2 yields an ordinary read-once word in a suitable variable order.
It can be viewed as an identity affine orbit; the order permutation is
already allowed by the model.

At gamma_i=t^i, C_y=t^(4e-2)=C_x^2. Every used and padded coordinate is
individually transcendental over the original coefficient field in every
characteristic. For N=0 the source separately uses the empty constant word
and does not apply the formula n=4N-2.

The source correctly says derivative separation only in characteristic
zero. There the derivatives i t^(i-1) are not merely nonzero but linearly
independent, because their degrees are distinct. In characteristic two
the y derivatives vanish, but the new all-rank theorem requires only
individual transcendence, which still holds. No small-characteristic
derivative-separation premise is asserted or needed.

4. LEADING SUPPORT AND DIGIT DEGREE

The exact residual scalar is
  product_e(2C_x X'_e-Y'_e+(X'_e)^2).
Every factor has nonzero linear part, since the Y'_e coefficient is -1.
The first nonzero ordinary homogeneous component is therefore
  product_e(2C_x X'_e-Y'_e),
of degree N. Every nonzero monomial chooses one coordinate from each
disjoint pair, so has support exactly N and exponents all equal to one.
Consequently its digit degree is also exactly N in every characteristic.
The all-Y coefficient is (-1)^N, proving nonvanishing even in
characteristic two, where it is the only monomial of this component.

Every monomial of the complete residual scalar still uses at least one
variable in each pair. Thus a fixed sink kills every coefficient row of
support below N but not the all-Y coefficient row. This proves the
coefficient-span lower statement over F(t), as well as the support and
digit-degree lower statements for the whole first component.

6. IMPORTANT LIMITS: AUGMENTATION AND SV

The source explicitly restricts interval preservation to the PHYSICAL
matrix word. For N>0 the scalar vanishes at C, so appending the scalar
endpoint matrices produces a full augmented interval with generic rank
one and evaluated rank zero. No augmented-rank preservation claim is made.
This is compatible with the all-rank PIT proof, whose later descent must
also preserve endpoint-augmented intervals before it can conclude that
the scalar has been hit.

The gamma+SV_1 survival claim is correct. For each factor its linear
amplitude coefficient is
  2C_x lambda_x(y)-lambda_y(y),
a nonzero old-field linear combination of two distinct Lagrange
polynomials (or just -lambda_y in characteristic two). Every factor is
nonzero after substitution; their product is nonzero in the polynomial
domain. Unshifted SV_1 also works, because z^2 lambda_x(y)^2 and
-z lambda_y(y) have different amplitude degrees and the latter is nonzero.

Accordingly this is not a lower bound for SV pairs, seed count, oracle
queries, or affine PIT. It is an exact support and digit-degree obstruction
for the WHOLE first homogeneous component at the specified actual shift.
The examples for compound-shaped palettes are not thereby shown to be
exterior powers of smaller matrix words.

prepared higher-rank chunk has original interval ranks three or four;
the compound width is six and its allowed nonzero ranks are three and

An actual local compound T=wedge^2 A obeys the Klein quadratic identity
  q(Tx)=det(A)q(x),
where q(x)=x_12 x_34-x_13 x_24+x_14 x_23. Its polarized bilinear form

quadratic identity must be retained; polarization alone loses information.
The rank-three images are the particular subspaces wedge^2 U with
dim U=3, not arbitrary three-dimensional subspaces.

In the natural active/passive 3+3 split, the abstract sharpness recursion
uses invertible passive gadgets diag(I_3,H). The J identity forces H to

Concrete target: prove or disprove an actual-compound first-component
