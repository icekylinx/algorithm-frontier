1. THE SUPPLIED AND ASSUMED THEOREMS ARE THE SAME

The companion's theorem labelled thm:cone-profile states:
  If every contiguous interval of a width-w product preserves its
  generic rank at zero, a nonzero full product of rank r admits a
  cone-preserving restriction to at most D_w(r) original blocks.

The affine addendum's Section 2 premise states the same assertion, for
a product on disjoint variable blocks, with the same recurrence
  D_w(w)=w^2-1,
  D_w(r)=2r^2(2 max_{r<s<=w} D_w(s)+1),  1<=r<w.
Its rank symbol s in the premise is merely a renaming of r. The companion
uses disjoint blocks throughout; singleton residual variables are a
special case. It imposes no individual-degree bound on this cone theorem.

Both sources define the same scalar-saturated image cone: over an algebraic
closure k of the current coefficient field, take the Zariski closure of
{lambda M(x)}. The scalar lambda is auxiliary and is not charged as an
original variable block. A coordinate restriction fixes entire unselected
original blocks to zero. The selected subset may depend on the product;
the black-box construction need not find it.

The companion does not restrict its theorem to base-field rational points.
This matters over finite fields. It explicitly uses an algebraic closure
of the CURRENT coefficient field, so the theorem can be invoked over the
old symbolic seed field of the affine induction.

2. GEOMETRIC PROOF SUPPLIED BY THE COMPANION

The proof does not invoke a width-three classification. Its ingredients
are the following field-independent matrix and image-closure facts.

(a) Invertible constants. Constant gauges normalize the factors to identity
constants. Prefix scalar-saturated cones are nested irreducible closed
subvarieties of Mat_w, starting at a line. A strict inclusion raises
dimension, so at most w^2-1 growth steps require a retained block.
Polynomial multiplication and density justify retaining the old restricted
image through a stable step and adding the current block at a growth step.

(b) Normalized equal-rank factors. For constant P=diag(I_r,0) and generic
rank r, a unit active pivot gives the rational Schur graph
  A=[I;D]E[I,C], E(0)=I, C(0)=D(0)=0.
The active prefix cones V_i are nested. At a stable step with the baseline
[V,0] contained in the preceding full cone, the source proves the exact
equality between the new cone and the closure of [T,0]A_i(a), T in V.

For fixed a in the nonempty open set where E(a) is invertible, stability
gives V E(a) contained in V. The left side is a closed irreducible variety
of the same dimension as V, so it equals V. The Schur graph then gives both
inclusions in the stable-step identity. Restricting current parameters to
that dense open set does not change the image closure. These facts hold
in every characteristic; no differential/separability assertion is used.

(c) Intrinsic rank. The same phase argument for an a-by-w starting matrix
with nonzero active part gives 2ar-1 remaining blocks. Retain the first
original block and work over k(X_1). Factor A_1=U_1 R_1 with r-column
U_1 of full column rank and R_1=[I_r,C_1]. Applying the rectangular bound
with a=r costs at most 2r^2-1 later blocks, hence 2r^2 including the first.

Crucially, the chosen subset is a single subset of original block indices
over the generic first-block field. It is not selected separately for each
minor or each specialization of X_1. If a homogeneous polynomial in the
output entries vanishes on that restricted image, cone equality over
k(X_1) makes it vanish on the full generic image, hence identically in
all original variables. Homogeneous vanishing ideals determine the closed
cones, so the same subset descends to the original algebraically closed
field. This supplies the uniform restriction needed by the addendum.

(d) Rank-profile induction. The companion's greedy decomposition is made
inside a full rank-r interval. Every subinterval has rank at least r.
Each shortest rank-r block is a higher-rank prefix followed by one local
factor; the remaining tail is higher-rank or empty. By induction, these
higher-rank pieces have cone-preserving restrictions. Absorbing the tail
costs at most 2 max_{s>r} D_w(s)+1 original blocks per macroblock.
The macroblocks and their adjacent products have constant rank r, so the
explicit simultaneous constant-gauge lemma normalizes them. The intrinsic
bound chooses at most 2r^2 macroblocks, giving exactly D_w(r).

Cone preservation composes across disjoint factors because products of
dense images are dense and matrix multiplication is polynomial. Independent
scalar amplitudes multiply into one scalar amplitude of the output.
Unselected macroblocks retain their original constant matrices when their
variables are set to zero. No extra compatibility hypothesis is omitted.

3. THE EXTERIOR-POWER INTERFACE IS JOINT AND SIMULTANEOUS

The companion's cone-composition lemma already states that a cone-preserving
restriction to t blocks makes EVERY exterior power coefficient-matrix
space t-concentrated. The affine addendum proves the same consequence
again as its Lemma 2.

A linear functional on the entries of wedge^r M is a homogeneous degree-r
polynomial in the entries of M. If it annihilates all coefficients of the
restriction, it vanishes on the restricted cone and thus on the full cone.
Equality of annihilators gives equality of the joint matrix coefficient
spaces. This does not merely prove a separate entrywise or rowwise claim.
Since the restriction consists of actual original coordinates, its
coefficients have support at most t. Equality after algebraic closure
descends by elementary field-extension linear algebra.

The affine proof invokes this at the ORIGINAL width w for higher-rank runs,
so b_r=max_{s>r}D_w(s) applies simultaneously to all their r-minors. It
does not substitute the much larger exterior dimension into D. This is
the precise interface responsible for the factorial-width estimate.

4. AFFINE BOOKKEEPING MATCHES THE CONE HYPOTHESES

At an affine rank-descent stage, the addendum uses the old-seed rational
function field E and independent latent residual coordinates Y_i. Each
local factor is
  A_i(Y_i+c_i+ell_i(G_old)).
It remains a polynomial in its own singleton Y_i. Translation over E is
a polynomial-ring automorphism, so generic interval ranks are unchanged.
Its value at Y=0 is exactly the previously prepared interval value.

Every interval inside a higher-rank run or tail has rank greater than the
current r, and hence already has its generic rank preserved at residual
zero. The cone theorem's premise therefore holds on each such run over
E, with no independence requirement on the different shift values.

Cone support concentration is converted to total-degree concentration
using local JOINT coefficient-matrix spans. Only selected local factors
are re-expanded; unselected constant coefficients stay unchanged. Distinct
singleton variables make each resulting product an actual coefficient.
Thus the higher-rank run has compound total-degree concentration eta_r b_r.
The single-block, adjacent-pair, and last-block/tail bounds are respectively
eta_r(b_r+1), eta_r(2b_r+2), and eta_r(2b_r+1).

A nonzero minor has a nonzero homogeneous component of degree at most the
stated bound. The residual substitution Y=LX is invertible and homogeneous,
so that component remains nonzero with the same degree. A degree-h monomial
has support at most h and is detected by a fresh SV_h in the ORIGINAL X
coordinates. This is an ordinary-total-degree transfer; it does not use
the invalid general transport of a low-digit latent witness.

All old seeds remain symbolic. Fresh amplitudes set to zero recover old
nonzero minors and the maintained local Wronskians. Adjacent rank-r block
products and the separate tail witness complete the interval induction.
Constant scalar endpoint matrices can be adjoined without creating new
variable coordinates or changing the local degree promise. Their role is
handled by the same interval argument, not a width-three scalar theorem.

5. WHERE THE CHARACTERISTIC RESTRICTION ENTERS

There is no characteristic restriction in the supplied cone theorem.
The affine addendum's degree-independent improvement uses its own local
Hasse-Wronskian lemma. For a polynomial matrix H of degree d and joint
coefficient-space dimension q, the coordinate polynomials are independent.
After an invertible basis change they have distinct degrees d_1<...<d_q.
The leading Hasse-Wronskian coefficient is, up to nonzero scalars,
  product_{mu<nu}(d_nu-d_mu) / product_{j=0}^{q-1} j!.
It is nonzero in characteristic zero or p>d, since q<=d+1.

For H=wedge^r A_i, d<=r delta and q<=binom(w,r)^2. Therefore the source's
uniform hypothesis char(F)=0 or p>w delta is sufficient at every r<w.
Under it, the local degree budget is
  eta_r=min{r delta, binom(w,r)^2-1}.

The initial SV_1 makes c_i+ell_i(SV_1)=c_i+z p_i(y) transcendental over the
original coefficient field because p_i is a nonzero Lagrange combination.
Thus every nonzero original-field local Wronskian survives. Correlation
among the shifts is harmless, and later symbolic additions preserve these
nonzero polynomial conditions by zero-amplitude recovery. No perfectness,
algebraic-closure oracle, or larger characteristic hypothesis is hidden.

In arbitrary characteristic the addendum instead uses eta_r=r delta.
That statement has degree-dependent seed count; it is not the same as a
degree-independent polynomial query bound for varying delta. The publication
should preserve this distinction. The later CP22 result supplies a
different all-characteristic degree-independent guarantee.

6. THE RETAINED FACTORIAL-WIDTH CONSEQUENCE

The companion establishes
  k_geo(w)=1+sum_{r<w}(2b_r+2)<=4^(w-1)(w!)^2 for w>=2.
The affine addendum then establishes, in characteristic zero or p>w delta,
  K=1+sum_{r<w}eta_r(2b_r+2)
   <=4^w k_geo(w)
   <=4^(2w-1)(w!)^2=2^{O(w log w)}.
At w=1, K=1. These constants are independent of delta in that regime.

The scalar has total degree at most n delta, and an SV coordinate has total
seed degree at most n. Hence the source's grid of at most
  (n^2 delta+1)^(2K)
queries is valid over a working field with at least n^2 delta+1 elements.
The working field must be an allowed oracle evaluation field. Field
extension does not change the characteristic condition.

The promise is local entry degree at most delta, or equivalently existence
of an unknown-order preimage ROABP with scalar individual degree at most
delta. The latter equivalence is legitimate HERE because factorwise
truncation preserves the scalar in disjoint variables and this theorem
allows arbitrary local rank patterns. It should not be exported to a
separate theorem promising only ranks 1 and w without rechecking that
pattern. Constants and n=0/delta=0 are explicitly separate cases.

The resulting width dependence is a legitimate retained good-characteristic
alternative to the newer 2^{O(w^2)} all-characteristic budget. It remains
an XP-style fixed-width polynomial query bound: the exponent depends on
w. It does not establish a uniform-exponent FPT bound.
