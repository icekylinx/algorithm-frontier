1. VERDICT AND ITS LIMITS

I did not identify a fatal mathematical flaw or an exact missing mathematical
assumption in this source. In particular, the intended objections concerning
future-dependent controls, loss of support through the zero reset, circular
same-level induction, correlated shifts in the Hasse argument, and the
adjacent-block rank descent did not produce a counterexample after explicit
reconstruction.

This is a limited negative finding from an internal model-assisted audit,
not certification of the theorem, not an independent human referee report,
and not a claim that a gap cannot remain. The weighted stable-phase lemma
is the central and least routine dependency. I reconstructed its quantitative
induction below rather than treating its statement as established.

I also constructed a width-four example in which the constant passive
product is zero but the corrected passive response is nonzero. The stated
controlled reset cancels that response exactly. Cleared-denominator identities
for this example were checked by exact sparse-polynomial arithmetic over
Z[b,x,y], so that calculation has no floating-point or characteristic-zero
division assumption. It is a stress test, not a proof of the general lemma.

2. DEPENDENCY STRUCTURE

The main claim depends on the following chain, all present in this source:

  (a) coefficient-span closure and unit-rank graph factorization;
  (b) the weighted stable-phase lemma, including positive-passive induction
      and zero-passive reset/source reduction;
  (c) palette row concentration in finite monomial Artin rings;
  (d) a separate box quotient for each selected leading monomial;
  (e) generic Hasse pivot closure and specialization at individually
      transcendental shifts;
  (f) detection of a leading component of bounded digit degree by SV;
  (g) descending preservation of all interval ranks using compound matrices,
      conditioned prepared chunks, adjacent-block tests, and a tail test;
  (h) interpolation using only the total degree of the original scalar.

The support bound and the exponent bound are genuinely separate. The proof
does not infer either one merely from the existence of a convenient
unfiltered coefficient. This separation is essential for its conclusion.

3. ARTIN COEFFICIENT SPANS AND UNIT-RANK GRAPHS
   Source lines 132-193.

The coefficient-span lemma is valid in the stated tensor product of finite
monomial quotients. In a product HF, every coefficient row is a field-linear
combination of coefficient rows of F. Multiplication by scalar polynomials
that involve variables occurring later in the word has the same property.
This is a statement about coefficient spans in the full tensor ring; it
does not assert preservation of support cost.

For a square H with invertible constant matrix, its inverse exists because
the augmentation ideal is nilpotent. Applying the inclusion to H and H^{-1}
gives equality of coefficient-row spans. The monomial quotient does not
identify a high monomial with a lower one, so the interpretation of actual
coefficients used later is preserved.

For an interval matrix with constant rank s and vanishing (s+1)-minors,
an s-by-s block with nonzero constant determinant is a unit. The bordered
minor identity then proves D=CE_0^{-1}B over the ring itself. Consequently
a row in the row module of that interval is determined by its active s
coordinates. This uses no fraction field of the Artin ring and remains
valid with zero divisors. The cases s=0 and s=W behave as asserted.

Constant invertible cutwise gauges telescope on intervals and preserve
the unit pivots and larger-minor identities. No polynomial gauge with
uncontrolled support is used for this step.

4. RECONSTRUCTION OF THE KERNEL DYNAMICS
   Source lines 217-287.

I interpret S_i, as required by the wording, as the coefficient-row span
obtained from S_0 by the first i matrices. Disjoint variable blocks imply
S_i=Coeff(S_{i-1} A_i). This equality is necessary here and is available
under the stated hypotheses.

Since the constant active block is I_a, constant-prefix propagation keeps
the active row matrix H unchanged. If the active projection of S_i is T,
then S_i decomposes as rowspan(B_i) plus its passive kernel. For d=rank H,
choose J with HJ=I_d. The active part of B_{i-1} A_i has all its coefficient
rows in T, so it equals R_i H with R_i(0)=I_d. In particular R_i is a unit
over the current block's Artin ring.

Writing B_{i-1,p}=B_p, the normalized row matrix has passive part

  (W_i)_p = H C_i + R_i^{-1} B_p F_i.

For p in K_{i-1}, active stability gives pD_iE_iJH=pD_iE_i. Therefore direct
subtraction yields

  (0,p)A_i - (pD_iE_iJ)W_i
    = (0, p(I-D_iE_iJR_i^{-1}B_p)F_i).

This is precisely the corrected passive operator in the manuscript. All
coefficients of the subtracted row belong to the available coefficient-row
span. Normalization by R_i and R_i^{-1} preserves the span. Subtracting
constant B_i rows then establishes both inclusions in the displayed kernel
recurrence, not merely one inclusion.

For a state a_{i-1} B_{i-1}+(0,p_{i-1}), the two evolution formulas follow by
the same calculation. They remain exact for polynomial scalar controls,
provided p_{i-1} is coefficientwise in K_{i-1}. The d=0 case is separate
and does not require a nonexistent right inverse.

The claim that each coefficient of Z_i has weighted prefix cost at most
one is also justified: B_{i-1} is the constant-prefix propagation of the
original zero-cost B_0, and R_i^{-1} uses only block i. Normalization can
raise the degree inside block i, but cannot introduce another block into
its support. Subtracting B_i costs zero.

5. FUTURE-DEPENDENT CONTROLS AND THE ZERO RESET
   Source lines 289-318.

Let Q_c...Q_e=0. The constant full interval is diag(I_a,0), so its assumed
unit rank is a and its active pivot is a unit. For fixed p in K_{c-1}, write
the active part of (0,p)P as L H. Write the active part of B_{c-1}P as
R H, with R(0)=I_d. Both coefficientwise factorizations follow from active
stability. The initial control

  a_{c-1} = -L R^{-1}

is defined in the interval tensor ring. It can depend on every interval
variable. Its controlled full output has zero active part; the row-graph
identity makes its entire output zero. The final active coordinate is zero
because H has a right inverse. Unrolling the exact passive evolution gives
the manuscript's reset, with no final Z_e term.

The future dependence is not an algebraic reachability obstruction. A
coefficientwise reachable state multiplied by an arbitrary scalar in the
full tensor ring still has every coefficient row in the same field-vector
space. Multiplication by the already traversed matrices preserves this
statement. At each cut, extracting and subtracting the active lift leaves
a coefficientwise passive remainder in K_i. This does not require the
control to be supported only on past variables.

However, it would be invalid to charge this control a bounded support
cost. The source explicitly avoids that inference. It uses the reset only
to obtain an unweighted coefficient-span inclusion and later pays for
sources using actual one-block witnesses. This distinction closes the
most obvious attempted attack on the proof.

6. POSITIVE-PASSIVE INDUCTION AND SUPPORT ACCOUNTING
   Source lines 319-363 and 391-400.

Suppose q=rank(Q_1...Q_n)>0 and b=a+q. The b here is the rank of the full
constant interval, hence belongs to the palette. The proposed persistent
passive core is valid. On U_i=U_0Q_1...Q_i, the suffix map is injective and
maps onto U_n. For V_i defined as the inverse image of V_n under that suffix
map, the induced map into the quotient by V_n is surjective on U_i.
Therefore dim V_i=k-q and U_i intersects V_i trivially. Each Q_i preserves
these splittings. Bases propagated along U_i make its constant action the
identity. Thus all constants can indeed be put in the enlarged block form
diag(I_a,I_q,Q'_i).

The enlarged active projections are nested: multiplication by the constant
part of the next matrix retains the same enlarged active coordinate for
every previously reachable row. Their dimensions start at d+beta and can
never exceed d+q, since projection onto the old a coordinates remains the
fixed d-dimensional T. Hence there are at most q-beta strict growth cuts.

Between these growth cuts, the hypotheses of the weighted lemma hold at
the strictly larger palette level b. The matrices are contiguous subwords
of the original matrices, not products of corrected passive operators.
Their interval rank identities therefore persist. A field-constant gauge
and a new initial reachable row space do not alter that fact.

The support recurrence is sound. If active lifts are linear combinations
of prefix coefficients of weighted cost at most L, and passive-kernel
generators have cost at most L+1, induction at level b spans a stable
subphase with cost at most L+G(b). Prefix witnesses and the subsequent
subphase variables occupy disjoint blocks, so multiplying their actual
coefficient witnesses adds support bounds. The next growth cut consumes
one new block and allows both the new lifts and new kernel generators
to be chosen within L+G(b)+1. Linear algebra selection and subtraction
do not increase that cost. Empty stable phases cause no difficulty.

If beta=0, the initial costs are 0 and 1. At most q growth cuts yield

  (q+1)G(b)+q = (q+1)(G(b)+1)-1 <= G(a).

If beta>=1, all enlarged active lifts can be assigned cost 1; the remaining
kernel has actual cost at most 1 and hence satisfies the allowed cost 2.
At most q-beta growth cuts yield

  1+(q-beta+1)G(b)+(q-beta)
    = (q-beta+1)(G(b)+1)
    <= q(G(b)+1) <= G(a).

The last inequality follows from
G(a)+1 >= (q+1)(G(b)+1), because G(b)>=0. There is no missing +1 in either
case. Every invocation in this positive-passive proof is at level b>a,
so this part is genuinely inductive on palette levels.

7. ZERO-PASSIVE SOURCE REDUCTION AND THE PAID SOURCE COST
   Source lines 365-389.

Take c maximal with Q_c...Q_n=0. Unrolling the kernel recurrence at c gives
the initial term K_{c-1} times the corrected interval and the sources
Z_j times their corrected suffixes for j>=c. Applying the reset to each
constant generator of K_{c-1} absorbs that initial term into the source
coefficient spans. Multipliers in the reset may be complicated, but scalar
coefficient-span closure is enough for this unweighted absorption.

Every suffix after a source j>=c has positive passive constant rank; the
empty suffix has rank k. Since Z_j uses only block j, the source response
is generated by responses from its individual coefficient rows p. Restart
the actual suffix from rowspan(B_j)+(0,span(p)). Its old active projection
is T: containment in global reachability supplies the upper bound, while
the constant-prefix B_j rows supply all of T. Its B_i and normalized
operators are the same as in the original evolution. The kernel recurrence
therefore contains every coefficient of p times the corrected suffix.

The already established positive-passive case supplies weighted budget
G(a) for this restart. Its B_j outputs use at most G(a) suffix blocks;
its p outputs use at most G(a)-1 suffix blocks. In the original prefix,
B_j costs zero and (0,p) costs at most one, by the local Z_j calculation.
The source block and suffix blocks are disjoint. Thus both output types
have total original weighted cost at most G(a).

In particular, no support bound on a reset control is being smuggled into
this last step. The same-level call is to the positive-passive case just
proved solely using higher levels; it is not a recursive call to the
unproved zero-passive case. I found no circularity here.

8. ROW CONCENTRATION AND THE BOX FILTER
   Source lines 403-463.

At a=0 with initial passive row u of weight one, the weighted bound
G(0) becomes ordinary support bound G(0)-1, exactly the displayed palette
product minus two. If the whole interval has unit rank zero, it is actually
the zero matrix in the Artin ring, so there is no exceptional nonzero
coefficient row.

For a selected nonzero leading coefficient g_alpha, the tensor box with
Y_i^(alpha_i+1)=0 on its support and all outside variables set to zero
has exactly the divisors of Y^alpha as surviving monomials. Every proper
divisor has degree below the first nonzero total degree. The scalar in
that box is consequently exactly g_alpha Y^alpha, with no additional
same-degree survivor.

Every original larger minor remains zero in the quotient, and a constant
nonzero pivot remains a unit. Thus row concentration applies in this box.
If alpha has too much support, every allowed small-support scalar
coefficient is zero, so contraction of the row-span conclusion forces
the surviving scalar coefficient to vanish. This is a valid contradiction.

The argument works separately for each leading monomial and therefore
bounds every such support. It does not rely on selecting a favorable
monomial that could be destroyed by the unknown linear transformation.
Arbitrary coefficient-field endpoints are legitimate, as long as they
are constant in the residual variables of the chunk.

9. HASSE PIVOTS, MOVING FUNCTIONALS, AND AFFINE DETECTION
   Source lines 465-587.

The pivot-closure proof uses the exact Hasse composition identity.
Applying D^[nu-mu] to a relation at mu produces on the right only indices
j+nu-mu-e<nu, since j<mu and e>=0. If binomial(nu,mu) is nonzero, the left
side cannot be a pivot. This proves closure under digit subindices in
positive characteristic. The number of those subindices is the product
of (digit+1), which is at least digit weight plus one, giving the stated
q-1 bound. Characteristic zero gives the usual consecutive order bound.

Individual transcendence of C over the original field is enough for the
moving-functional lemma. All rational jet relations and all nonzero finite
jet minors over that original field specialize without a zero denominator
or a loss of rank. The functional may have coefficients in a much larger
field and depend on C or other correlated shifts. Such dependence does
not invalidate an already specialized linear relation. No joint algebraic
independence of the shifts is needed for this argument.

For a leading monomial, fixing the other exponents gives a matrix-entry
functional of the remaining local Hasse derivative. Every earlier order
vanishes because its full total degree is lower. The nonzero selected
order is thus a generic pivot of the original vector of W^2 entries.
This proves the per-coordinate digit-weight bound for every leading
monomial, independently of rank preservation.

Under an invertible residual linear map, a monomial of digit degree at
most k expands into monomials of digit degree at most k. Frobenius handles
each digit copy; exponent collisions can decrease, but cannot increase,
digit weight. Consequently support is at most k. The SV image contains
the coordinate subspace corresponding to the support of any retained
nonzero monomial. Higher ordinary homogeneous degrees have larger fresh
amplitude degrees and cannot cancel the detected first component.

10. DESCENDING RANK INVARIANT, CONDITIONING, AND QUANTITATIVE CLAIMS
    Source lines 589-797.

The augmented endpoints turn the whole scalar into a rank-one matrix
interval without adding residual variables. Each latent C_i is individually
transcendental because its nonzero t-polynomial cannot be canceled by the
SV terms, which use independent seeds. This remains true in characteristic
p even if its ordinary derivative vanishes.

Every rank-w interval initially survives: each factor is full rank, its
nonzero univariate determinant survives at its individually transcendental
argument, and determinants multiply. A previously nonzero minor remains
nonzero after adding new pairs because setting their amplitudes to zero
recovers the preceding shift.

Inside a fixed generic-rank-r target interval, every subinterval has rank
at least r. Therefore the greedy shortest-rank-r prefixes cannot jump
below r. Their proper-prefix chunks and the leftover tail have rank greater
than r, and so do all subintervals within them. Each listed block,
adjacent-block product, and final block-tail product has rank exactly r.

The compound rank of a generic-rank-s matrix is binomial(s,r) over any
field. Shifted chunks have the same generic rank as before translation,
and their constants have the same rank by the inductive invariant.
Their larger-minor identities therefore give precisely the palette used
in the leading-support theorem. The first gap is r+1, and subsequent
gaps are binomial(s-1,r-1), yielding the displayed h_{w,r}.

Conditioning outside a prepared chunk is legitimate because each matrix
has its own variable block and the chunk is contiguous. Extracting fixed
outside coefficients leaves a fixed left row and right column. If a lower
total chunk degree survived with those endpoints, it would be a lower
total degree of the full test. Thus the chosen chunk monomial really is
in its first homogeneous component. Endpoint dependence on old seeds
does not change this conclusion.

There are at most two prepared chunks and two singleton layers in any
selected test. The support bound and independent exponent bound apply
to the same arbitrary leading monomial, so their product bounds the
digit degree of the entire first component. A single fresh symbolic SV
map therefore preserves every selected test. Finiteness is enough for
simultaneous symbolic nonzeroness; no finite-field union bound or seed
specialization is hidden here.

The chain lemma is valid: a rank-r prefix has the same row space as its
last rank-r block, and preservation of the adjacent product transfers
that property to the next block. The separately counted tail test
completes the argument. No three-prepared-chunk test has been omitted.
Descending to r=1 preserves the nonzero scalar augmented interval.

The final interpolation bounds use only deg f<=D: the t degree is at most
nD, each amplitude degree at most D, and each direction degree at most
(n-1)D. Unbounded local entry degrees do not change those scalar bounds.
The permission for extension-field evaluations is an explicit oracle
assumption in the theorem and is needed when the base field is too small.
The pure-SV first-pair specialization gives t times a nonzero Lagrange
combination and therefore supplies the same individual transcendence.

11. NONTRIVIAL RESET STRESS TEST

Take width W=4, active size a=1, variables x and y in separate blocks, and

  A_1 = ((1,0,0,x), (0,1,x,0), (0,0,0,0), (0,0,0,0)),
  A_2 = ((1,0,0,0), (y,0,0,0), (0,0,0,y), (0,0,0,1)),

where the displayed tuples are rows. Each local matrix has unit rank 2.
Their product has rows

  (1,0,0,x), (y,0,0,xy), (0,0,0,0), (0,0,0,0),

and has unit rank 1. Thus all nonempty intervals preserve their constant
rank. Let B_0=(1,b,0,0), with b a field scalar, and let the initial passive
kernel contain p=(1,0,0). The old active projection is the one-dimensional
space T and is automatically stable.

The passive constants are Q_1=diag(1,0,0) and Q_2=diag(0,0,1), so Q_1Q_2=0.
The corrected passive matrices in this example are

  Ftilde_1 = ((1,x,0), (0,0,0), (0,0,0)),
  Ftilde_2 = ((0,0,0), (0,0,y), (0,0,1)).

Here Z_1=(0,bx,x), Z_2=0, and

  p Ftilde_1 Ftilde_2 = (0,0,xy) != 0,
  Z_1 Ftilde_2 = (0,0,x(1+by)).

The future-dependent initial control a_0=a_1=-y/(1+by) kills the entire
controlled product, and the reset reads

  (0,0,xy) = -a_1 Z_1 Ftilde_2.

The denominator is a unit in every relevant Artin quotient. After clearing
it, both this identity and the controlled full-product identity are
polynomial identities over Z[b,x,y]. Exact sparse-polynomial checks passed.
The example specifically rules out simplifying the argument to the false
claim that a zero passive constant product forces the corrected passive
product itself to be zero. The actual source does not make that claim.

12. REMAINING REVIEW RISK AND PRESENTATION SUGGESTIONS

No missing premise was established in this audit. The following would make
future human verification easier, but I do not regard them as repairs to a
demonstrated mathematical defect:

  * Define S_i explicitly as Coeff(S_0 A_1...A_i) and state once why disjoint
    blocks identify it with Coeff(S_{i-1}A_i).
  * State explicitly that coefficientwise membership in a field-vector
    subspace is preserved by multiplication by arbitrary scalars in the
    full tensor ring. This makes the future-control justification easier
    to verify without interpreting it as a weighted assertion.
  * Give a separate short witness-substitution observation for the stable
    subphase support recurrence. It should say that field-linear changes
    of prefix generators preserve a budget, and multiplication by actual
    coefficients on disjoint later blocks adds the budgets.
  * Write the explicit reset control -L R^{-1}; this highlights where the
    unit hypothesis is used and where future-variable dependence enters.
  * Include a small nonzero-response reset example such as the one above,
    if explanatory space permits.

These are exposition suggestions only. The exact source already supplies
the required unit pivots, disjoint block structure, weighted initial costs,
individual transcendence, finite symbolic seed fields, and extension-oracle
assumption. The unresolved limitation is the ordinary limitation of this
internal audit: it is not external peer review or a formal machine-checked
proof, and its failure to find an error cannot establish correctness.
