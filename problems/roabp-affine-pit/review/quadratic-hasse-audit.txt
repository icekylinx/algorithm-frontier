1. DETERMINANT IDENTITY

Set N=w^2-1 and k_j=wj. Factoring

  c_j=N!/[(k_j+w-1)!(N-k_j)!]

from column j of binom(N,k_j+i) leaves

  P_i(k)=product_{a=i+1}^{w-1}(k+a)
         product_{b=0}^{i-1}(N-k-b).

Indeed multiplying this expression by c_j cancels the two factorial
tails and gives N!/[(k_j+i)!(N-k_j-i)!]. All these arguments are
nonnegative for the actual k_j=wj and 0<=i,j<w.

Each P_i has degree at most w-1. At q_s=N-s, the second product vanishes
when i>s, and

  P_s(q_s)=s! (N+w-1-s)!/N!.

Thus the w-by-w evaluation matrix (rows i, columns s) is triangular
with the displayed nonzero diagonal. Its Vandermonde is

  product_{a<b}(q_b-q_a)
   =(-1)^[w(w-1)/2] product_{s=0}^{w-1}s!.

The resulting determinant of the coefficient matrix of P_i is

  (-1)^[w(w-1)/2] product_{s=0}^{w-1}(N+s)!/(N!)^w.

Evaluating the same coefficient basis at k_j and restoring the c_j
gives exactly source equation (1). This argument is an identity over
the rationals at the required integer k_j. Clearing the integer
factorial denominators allows reduction modulo p.

Every relevant factorial argument is at most N+w-1, which is strictly
below p. Each Vandermonde difference is w(b-a), a positive integer
at most w(w-1)<p. Hence every numerator and denominator factor is a
unit modulo p, and the binomial determinant remains nonzero. Merely
having a nonzero rational determinant would not suffice; the explicit
factorization supplies the required reduction argument.

2. THE MIDDLE MATRIX AND ITS ENTRY SPACE

The integers i+wj biject with 0,...,N. Since p>N, every binomial(N,m)
is nonzero modulo p. Thus the entries of A(T) are nonzero scalar
multiples of the distinct monomials T^0,...,T^N. Their coefficient
space has dimension w^2, and the generic local Hasse pivots are
0,...,N in this characteristic range.

In every determinant term the T exponent and sign exponent coincide:

  E=wN-sum_i i-w sum_j j=w(w^2-1)/2.

The latter is an integer, since w(w-1)(w+1) is even. Factoring the
common monomial and common sign from the determinant expansion gives

  det A(T)=(-1)^E T^E det[binom(N,i+wj)].

It is nonzero. At every transcendental C it remains nonzero.
The contraction u(C)A(T)v(C) is the full binomial expansion of
(C-T)^N, because its (i,j) entry contributes C^(i+wj) times the
coefficient of that exponent. There are no repeated or omitted terms.

3. ORIGINAL-FIELD ENDPOINT REALIZATION

U(X) differs from the identity only in off-diagonal positions of its
first row; V(Z) differs only in off-diagonal positions of its first
column. Both are unipotent with determinant one, over the original
field polynomial rings. Their source row and sink column supply
X^(ip) and Z^(jp), respectively. No coefficient depends on the later
shift parameter, and X,T,Z are three distinct variables, each used
in one local matrix.

The original scalar monomials have distinct X and Z exponent pairs,
so no cancellation can invalidate the degree calculation. For (i,j),
the total degree is N+i(p-1)+j(p-w). Both slopes are positive. The
unique maximum occurs at i=j=w-1, has coefficient one, and is
2p(w-1). This also proves that the scalar polynomial is nonzero.

4. THE ACTUAL CURVE AND ALL PHYSICAL INTERVALS

For n=p, the coordinate indices 1,w,p are distinct because 2<=w<p.
The assignment X=X_1, T=X_p, Z=X_w is therefore a valid unknown-order
ROABP in n observed variables; other coordinates have identity layers.
At gamma_i=t^i, write C=t^p. Then X^p=C, T=C, Z^p=C^w.
The middle contraction is (C-C)^N=0, while every local matrix is
invertible over F(t). Hence every product of consecutive physical
layers is invertible, exactly as is its generic counterpart.

The scalar can vanish while the full matrix product remains invertible.
If constant scalar endpoint matrices are appended, their full interval
does drop rank; the source explicitly excludes that augmented interval
from its rank-preservation assertion.

5. ENTIRE FIRST COMPONENT, NOT ONLY A SELECTED COEFFICIENT

Frobenius gives (t+Y_1)^p=C+Y_1^p and
(t^w+Y_w)^p=C^w+Y_w^p. Every nonconstant endpoint contribution has
residual ordinary degree at least p>N. Multiplying by the polynomial
middle matrix cannot lower that degree. Thus ALL residual terms of
degree at most N arise from the constant endpoint row and column.
Their exact contraction is

  u(C)A(C+Y_p)v(C)=(-Y_p)^N.

This proves both absence of all lower homogeneous terms and the exact
whole first homogeneous component. Since 0<N<p, its digit degree is N.
No unfiltered coefficient concentration or moving-endpoint Hasse lemma
is being used to infer this conclusion.

The middle curve argument t^p has derivative zero. Consequently the
example does not satisfy the stronger premise that every relevant
original-field latent coordinate has nonzero derivative. Moreover the
local degree of U and V is (w-1)p, so the CP19 condition p>delta does
not apply either.

One fresh SV pair does hit this example: the amplitude-degree-N
coefficient is (-lambda_p(y))^N, nonzero, and endpoint variations
have amplitude degree at least p>N. This proves the claimed negative
scope of the obstruction: it is not a generator lower bound.
