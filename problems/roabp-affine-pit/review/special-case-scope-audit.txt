1. COMMON MODEL AND NOTATION

The scalar is f(X)=u product_i A_i(ell_i(X)+c_i) v, with one unknown
read-once order and an unknown INVERTIBLE affine change of n coordinates.
The ell_i are the independent coordinate forms of that change. This is
not the stronger any-order promise, not a singular affine projection, and
not an orbit closure. Local matrices are over the original coefficient
field, not over a later seed field. D denotes a bound on scalar total
degree; delta denotes the degree promise specified separately in each row.

SV_k has k independent Lagrange pairs (z_j,y_j), hence 2k parameters.
gamma+SV_k has 2k+1 parameters. Extension-field oracle evaluations are
required if the base field cannot supply the stated evaluation sets.
Every fixed-width polynomial-time statement is XP in the width parameter;
no fixed-parameter tractability claim is made. Constants need one query.

2. NONREDUNDANT RESULT CARDS

A. CP22: arbitrary local ranks, every characteristic, scalar degree D.

  W_r=binom(w,r),
  h_r=(r+2) product_{s=r+2}^w (binom(s-1,r-1)+1)-2,
  k_r=(W_r^2-1)(2h_r+2),
  K(w)=sum_{r=1}^{w-1} k_r; K(1)=0.

  Map: gamma_i=t^i plus SV_K; also pure SV_(K+1).
  K(1),...,K(6)=0,12,128,1500,23718,553854.
  Safe asymptotic K=2^{O(w^2)}. No bound on local representation degrees
  is needed; the final finite grid depends on scalar total degree D.
  Refined query count:

    (nD+1)[(D+1)B]^K,
    B=min{ (n-1)(n+2)(n+3)/2+1, (n-1)D+1 }.

  Field size nD+1 suffices, for n,D>=1. This is the broad all-rank,
  all-characteristic candidate, but it does NOT supersede the smaller
  bounds under the additional promises in cards B--E.

B. CP13: arbitrary local ranks; factorial width dependence in good
   characteristic, with a cone-theorem dependency that must be supplied.

  Define D_w(w)=w^2-1 and
    D_w(r)=2r^2(2 max_{s>r} D_w(s)+1),
    b_r=max_{s>r}D_w(s),
    k_geo=1+sum_{r<w}(2b_r+2).
  In char 0 or char p>w*delta, set
    eta_r=min{r*delta, binom(w,r)^2-1},
    K_good=1+sum_{r<w} eta_r(2b_r+2).
  Pure SV_(K_good) hits, with

    K_good <= 4^(2w-1)(w!)^2 = 2^{O(w log w)}  (w>=2),
    K_good=1 (w=1).

  This asymptotic width bound can improve on CP22, so it must not be
  discarded merely because it predates CP22. It need not be numerically
  smaller at each small width.

  Here delta may be a latent SCALAR individual-degree bound. Truncating
  the local matrices above delta preserves that scalar, and arbitrary
  local ranks remain allowed. The original affine output has total degree
  at most n*delta. The stated finite grid uses
    (n^2*delta+1)^(2K_good) queries,
  over a field with at least n^2*delta+1 elements.

  In every characteristic the same addendum uses eta_r=r*delta, with
    K <= delta*w*4^(w-1)(w!)^2 (w>=2).
  This is polynomial for fixed w AND delta; unlike CP22, its exponent
  is not independent of delta in small characteristic.

  The CP13 addendum is explicitly conditional on the companion
  rank-profile IMAGE-CONE theorem. Publishing the addendum alone is not
  a complete proof of the factorial bound. Include the focused interval-
  rank manuscript, whose geometric section supplies that theorem and
  whose earlier sections supply its normalization/rank-descent machinery.
  Its elementary fallback gives 2^{O(w^2)}, not the factorial improvement.

C. CP18: width at most three, arbitrary local ranks, every characteristic.

  Let e_i=i in char 0; in char p let e_i be the ith positive integer
  not divisible by p. Put rho=e_n<=2n-1. The map
    t^(e_i)+SV_4,i
  has nine parameters; pure SV_5 also hits. This is much smaller than
  substituting w=3 in CP22, and includes the rank-two layers excluded by
  CP21's width-three rank-one/full specialization.

  The frozen theorem allows delta to be a latent scalar individual-degree
  bound, by local truncation, since no intermediate-rank promise is lost.
  With D=n*delta, its direction addendum gives
    n^8 (rho D+1)(D+1)^4 = O(n^14 delta^5)
  queries, field size rho D+1. The basic pure-SV_5 grid is
    (n^2*delta+1)^10.
  The complete proof is independent of the general cone theorem and of
  the later CP22 Artin/palette proof.

D. CP19: local generic ranks one or w, char 0 or char p>delta.

  Map: ordinary moment curve gamma_i=t^i plus SV_(w-1), with 2w-1
  parameters; also pure SV_w. The whole first scalar component of an
  invertible segment has ORDINARY degree at most w-1.
  With D=n*delta, the refined query count is
    n^(2w-2)(nD+1)(D+1)^(w-1)
      = O_w(n^(3w-1) delta^w),
  field size nD+1.

  CRUCIAL DEGREE PROMISE: delta bounds entries in an actual representation
  with the asserted rank-one/full pattern. A scalar individual-degree
  promise alone cannot replace it, because truncation can destroy that
  local rank pattern. There is no p>w, p>w*delta, or p>n*delta requirement;
  p>delta is the exact finite-characteristic hypothesis in this source.
  Width one itself works in all characteristics.

  This remains useful for general w: its w-1 pairs are smaller than
  CP21's b(w) from width five onward. At widths two through four CP21
  gives the same pair count without the good-characteristic restriction.

E. CP21: local generic ranks one or w, every characteristic, scalar degree D.

  b(1)=0;
  b(w)=max_{1<=k<=w-1}{binom(w-2,k-1)+k-1}, w>=2.
  b(2),...,b(8)=1,2,3,5,8,13,23.
  Use the derivative-separating exponents e_i from card C, rho=e_n.
  Map: gamma_i=t^(e_i) plus SV_b; also pure SV_(b+1).
  Query count:
    n^(2b)(rho D+1)(D+1)^b,
  field size max{n^2,rho D+1}. For D=n*delta this is
    O_w(n^(3b+2) delta^(b+1)).

  There is no characteristic or local matrix degree restriction. A known
  latent scalar individual-degree delta implies D<=n*delta WITHOUT
  truncating or changing the promised rank-one/full representation.
  The entire first homogeneous component, not just one coefficient,
  has characteristic digit degree at most b(w).

  Exact recovered special cases:
    CP17, width two: gamma+SV_1, three parameters; pure SV_2.
    Width three, ranks one/three only: gamma+SV_2; pure SV_3.
    CP20, width four, ranks one/four only: gamma+SV_3; pure SV_4.
  All widths at most two are covered by its width-two case. Rank-three
  layers at width four are NOT covered. CP17/CP20 source histories and
  audits can be archived, but need not be duplicated in the minimal
  canonical proof set, because CP21 proves these exact specializations.

4. CURVE AND LOWER-BOUND WARNINGS FOR THE PUBLIC SUMMARY

The ordinary moment curve t^i makes every nonzero original-field affine
coordinate form transcendental in every characteristic. It does NOT
make every such form have nonzero derivative in positive characteristic.
CP22's Hasse filtering uses only individual transcendence. CP21 and CP18
use the stronger derivative-separating curve t^(e_i); CP19 uses ordinary
derivatives plus the local representation-degree restriction.

Do not present min{b(w),(w-1)(w^2-1)} as a first-component bound under
only individual transcendence. The new original-field width-two example
at an ordinary moment curve has first component -Y_p^3 in char p>=5;
the new general-width supplement raises this obstruction to w^2-1.
Both fail derivative separation. Neither is a lower bound on the number
of SV pairs: one additional pair detects these particular examples.

Do not silently replace representation-degree delta by scalar degree in
CP19, weaken p>w*delta to p>delta in CP13, or drop the cone dependency
from CP13's factorial bound. Conversely, CP22/CP21 do not require any
bound on representation degrees when scalar total degree D is known.

The comparison literature should name Vishwas Bhargava and Sumanta Ghosh.
Their any-order and unknown-order theorems are different promises. Keep
research-validity status separate from literature/priority status.
