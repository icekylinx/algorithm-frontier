1. MODEL AND RESULT

The original scalar polynomial has degree at most D and lies in an unknown
invertible affine orbit of an unknown-order width-w ROABP. All univariate
entry degrees and local generic ranks are allowed. The structural theorem
supplies gamma+SV_K, gamma_i=t^i, K=K(w), with rank-stage budgets k_r.

Let M=(n+2)(n+3)/2 and Q=(n-1)M+1. The addendum establishes that each of the
K direction coordinates can use any common Q-element set while the initial
parameter and amplitudes use sets of sizes nD+1 and D+1. The count is
  Q^K(nD+1)(D+1)^K,
over a field of size at least max{Q,nD+1}. For fixed w this is
O_w(n^(3K+1)D^(K+1)). The direct inequality Q<=2n^3 holds also at n=1.

The formal source then chooses globally
  B=min{Q,(n-1)D+1}
and obtains
  (nD+1)[(D+1)B]^K
over a working field of size at least nD+1. The latter uses one complete
argument or the other, not an unsupported stagewise hybrid.

2. MAXIMUM-DIGIT-WEIGHT POLARIZATION

Let H be the nonzero first ordinary homogeneous component of a scalar
test, of ordinary degree m and characteristic digit degree at most k.
Choose a monomial of MAXIMUM digit weight k_0 among H's monomials, and
list its k_0 digit powers q_j, with repetitions. Their sum is m. In
characteristic zero take all q_j=1.

Consider the coefficient of product_j z_j^(q_j) in
H(sum_j z_j V_j). Every positive target amplitude needs at least one digit
copy. No source monomial has more than k_0 copies. Thus any contributor
uses exactly one copy per amplitude; a carry formed from several copies
inside one amplitude cannot occur. The resulting coefficient is a tensor
separately linear in the Frobenius coordinates V_(j,i)^(q_j).

Assigning the selected monomial's copies to their own direction entries
gives coefficient h_alpha times product_(i,e) alpha_(i,e)!, nonzero because
each base-p digit is less than p. In characteristic zero the factor is
product_i alpha_i!, also nonzero. A different source exponent vector
cannot give this same direction monomial, since summing its direction
exponents reconstructs alpha. Therefore the tensor is nonzero. There is
no division by m!, and no implicit assumption m<p.

The maximum-weight choice is essential and is stated explicitly. A
lower-weight selected monomial would allow other terms' digit copies to
carry into the requested amplitudes and would not prove the special tensor
form used in the root-count argument.

3. ROOT COUNTS OVER THE OLD-SEED COEFFICIENT FIELD

For each q_j, the n functions lambda_i(y)^(q_j) are linearly independent
over any coefficient-field extension. The Lagrange coefficient matrix is
invertible, and entrywise Frobenius preserves its nonzero determinant;
equivalently, evaluation at the original Lagrange nodes gives the identity.
Tensoring these injective substitutions preserves the nonzero tensor.

With the other directions viewed as formal, a nonzero tensor as a function
of y_j is a nonzero linear combination of lambda_i(y_j)^(q_j). Over an
algebraic closure of that coefficient field it is a q_j-th power of a
polynomial of degree at most n-1. Thus at most n-1 distinct elements of
the working field can annihilate it identically in all other directions.
The coefficient field may contain t, old amplitudes, and other directions.
The proof neither assumes a perfect base field nor asks the algorithm to
extract roots.

For M_0 tests, choose a tensor for each nonconstant test. At each successive
direction, at most (n-1)M_0 values are forbidden, so any prescribed set
of that size plus one contains a value preserving all current tensors.
Unused directions and tests with nonzero constant term impose no condition.
Previously fixed directions only change coefficients in the still
separately Frobenius-linear tensor. Higher ordinary homogeneous components
have larger total amplitude degree and cannot cancel the chosen tensor.

The selection is existential for an explicit Cartesian hitting grid. The
algorithm never needs to know the representation, minors, tensors, or good
direction values.

4. COUNTING DISTINCT RANK CONDITIONS

The companion augments the word with two scalar-endpoint factors, for a
total of N=n+2 factors. There are exactly N(N+1)/2=M possible nonempty
contiguous intervals. At a rank-r stage, a target decomposition requires
only individual greedy blocks, adjacent-block products, and last-block/
tail products. Every such object is one of those contiguous intervals.

Deduplicate the required tests by interval over all target decompositions
and choose one nonzero r-minor for each. A single surviving r-minor proves
rank at least r; larger minors vanish identically because that interval
has generic rank r. The same chosen minor therefore certifies all its
occurrences. The companion bounds EVERY nonzero r-minor of a required
test, so any one decomposition witnessing the interval's admissible shape
supplies the leading-component bound for this chosen minor.

There is no extra multiplicative factor for the number of minors, target
intervals containing the test, or possible greedy decompositions. Hence
M is a valid simultaneous condition count at each stage.

5. FIX DIRECTIONS WITHIN THE RANK DESCENT

The argument correctly fixes directions stage by stage, retaining t and
all old amplitudes as independent variables. The current coefficient field
is E(t,old amplitudes). Each latent shift remains
  c_i+ell_i(t,t^2,...,t^n)+a polynomial in old amplitudes.
Its t-part is nonconstant because ell_i is a nonzero row of the invertible
linear transformation. Therefore each shift is transcendental over the
original coefficient field, and over E, in every characteristic. Ordinary
derivatives are not needed; t^p is just as transcendental as t.

The new direction choices preserve every new scalar minor as a polynomial
in the old formal parameters and the fresh amplitudes. Setting fresh
amplitudes to zero recovers all old nonzero higher-rank minors. The same
adjacent-block row-space argument used in the companion then restores the
rank invariant at the new stage.

Proof gauges, unit pivots, and coefficient bases are chosen anew over the
current old-parameter field. There is no requirement to preserve a
particular earlier rational gauge formula under later direction fixing.
The actual tests are minors of original polynomial intervals. Their
analysis coefficients lie in the current coefficient field, with no
denominator involving the NEW directions being selected. This avoids a
hidden denominator condition beyond the counted interval tests.

After all stages, at least one direction tuple in the stated Cartesian
grid leaves the original scalar nonzero as a polynomial in t and the
amplitudes. Its degree in t is at most nD, and in each amplitude at most
D. Ordinary interpolation on those parameters proves the advertised query
count. Entry degrees and auxiliary-minor degrees do not enter this final
step.

6. ALTERNATIVE INITIAL CURVE AND PURE SV

With eta_i(t)=t lambda_i(t), every nonzero latent combination is c+t q(t)
for nonzero q, so it is nonconstant in all characteristics and has degree
at most n. The full stagewise proof is unchanged. This map is the image
of pure SV_(K+1) with its first pair set to (t,t).

The result is a subfamily of the pure generator's image; it does not claim
that the initial t values must belong to the Q-element direction set used
for the remaining pairs. No additional field restriction is needed.

7. MINIMUM-GRID COROLLARY ADDED IN THE FORMAL SOURCE

If Q<=(n-1)D+1, the root-count construction applies to all directions and
Q<=nD+1, so its field requirement reduces to nD+1. If the inequality is
reversed, use ordinary interpolation of the full nonzero substituted
scalar from the companion theorem. Its direction degrees are at most
(n-1)D, amplitude degrees at most D, and initial-parameter degree at most
nD. This gives the other branch with the same field bound.

The branch is selected once for the entire generator. All directions use
the same size-B set justified by that complete argument. This establishes
the precise count (nD+1)[(D+1)B]^K without mixing a condition count from one
stage with a scalar-degree promise that does not bound auxiliary tests.
The corollary is valid for both gamma and eta.

8. SCOPE, EDGES, AND FINAL ASSESSMENT

The n,w,D>=1 domain is explicit. Nonzero constants need one query, and
n=1 admits the separately stated elementary D+1 univariate bound. At w=1,
K=0 and the general formula specializes consistently. The evaluation sets
may overlap, and nD+1 supplies the n Lagrange nodes. Extension-field access
is expressly required if the working field extends the oracle's base
field. The affine map remains invertible, and the result does not cover
singular projections or unrestricted repeated factors.

The fixed-width asymptotic notation O_w is correct. The direction
refinement decreases the scalar-degree exponent of the degree-independent
branch to K+1, while its n-dependence is explicit and larger. No claim of
optimality, a lower bound, or an FPT exponent uniform in w is made.

No mathematical repair is requested. The condition deduplication, maximal-
weight digit tensor, arbitrary-coefficient-field root count, and stagewise
reconstruction of proof gauges are all essential and all present in the
audited exact source.
