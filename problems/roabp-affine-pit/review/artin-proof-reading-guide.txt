This note spells out four elementary interfaces used in the frozen
candidate manuscript. It does not change its hypotheses, conclusions,
constants, or source. It is an explanatory companion to an internal
model-assisted candidate proof, not external human peer review or a
formal verification of the complete theorem. Lemma names below refer to
the frozen source and remain usable if its rendered numbering changes.

1. WHAT THE REACHABLE COEFFICIENT SPACE S_i MEANS
   Weighted stable-phase lemma; source lines 217-244.

Let R_j=E[X_j]/I_j be the finite monomial rings of the manuscript. Choose
an E-basis r_1,...,r_s of the initial row space S_0. For i>=0, define

  P_i=A_1(X_1)...A_i(X_i), with P_0=I,
  S_i=span_E{ [X^alpha](r_h P_i) : 1<=h<=s,
                                      alpha survives in R_1 tensor ... tensor R_i }.

Here a coefficient is a row vector over E. The definition is independent
of the chosen basis of S_0. It is also equivalent to taking coefficients
from every initial row in S_0.

Because block i is disjoint from all earlier blocks, coefficient
extraction gives the useful exact equality

  S_i = Coeff(S_{i-1} A_i).

Indeed, if r is a coefficient row of r_h P_{i-1} and M is a coefficient
matrix of A_i, then rM is the coefficient of r_h P_i at the concatenation
of those two monomials. Conversely, every coefficient of r_h P_i has
that form. Passing to field-linear spans proves the equality.

For the weighted lemma, use initial rows of B_0 with initial cost 0 and
a basis of (0,K_0) with initial cost 1. The cost of an actual prefix
coefficient is its initial-row cost plus the number of blocks in its
support. A row has a witness of cost at most L if it belongs to the
E-linear span of such actual coefficients of cost at most L.

The active-stability assumption says that the projection of S_i onto
the first a coordinates is the same space T for every i. The propagated
constant row matrix B_i=B_0 A_1(0)...A_i(0) has the same independent
active row matrix H as B_0. Its rows therefore supply a lift of T inside
S_i. If

  K_i={p in E^(W-a) : (0,p) belongs to S_i},

then

  S_i=rowspan_E(B_i) direct-sum (0,K_i).

For any row of S_i, subtract its unique active lift from rowspan(B_i).
The remainder is passive. Directness follows from independence of the
rows of H. This justifies the kernel decomposition used in the source.

2. SCALAR COEFFICIENT-SPAN CLOSURE, INCLUDING FUTURE VARIABLES
   Coefficient-span lemma and zero-reset paragraph;
   source lines 146-160 and 311-317.

Let R be the full tensor ring, let V be a fixed E-vector subspace of
E^W, and let F be a row polynomial whose every coefficient belongs to V.
For any scalar h in R, every coefficient of hF belongs to V as well.

To see this directly, write h=sum_beta h_beta X^beta and
F=sum_alpha F_alpha X^alpha in the surviving monomial basis. Each
coefficient of the product is a sum of terms h_beta F_alpha. Terms
killed by the monomial ideal are discarded. Every retained term is an
E-scalar multiple of a vector of V. This argument allows overlap between
the variable sets of h and F and allows h to involve variables that
occur later in the matrix word.

The same reasoning works for a row family multiplied on the left by a
compatible polynomial matrix: each output coefficient row is an
E-linear combination of input coefficient rows. If the left matrix is
square and has invertible constant term, its inverse exists in R.
Applying the inclusion first to the matrix and then to its inverse
proves equality of coefficient-row spans.

This is an UNWEIGHTED statement. Scalar multiplication can introduce
many supported blocks. The statement does not preserve a support budget.
The reset uses it only to show membership in S_i or K_i and to absorb
responses into coefficient spans. The later source-restart argument
obtains its support bound from actual prefix and suffix witnesses.

For example, a controlled intermediate state is a scalar combination
of rows propagated from a reachable initial row. Its coefficients lie
in the appropriate S_i by the inclusion just proved. If its active
coordinate row is a_i, subtracting a_i B_i leaves a row with zero active
part whose coefficients are still in S_i. Its passive coefficient rows
therefore belong to K_i. This is the precise meaning of coefficientwise
reachability for a control that may depend on future variables.

3. WHY PREFIX AND SUFFIX WITNESS COSTS ADD
   Positive-passive induction and source restart;
   source lines 340-360 and 374-387.

Fix a cut j. Suppose a row r has a prefix witness

  r=sum_nu c_nu [X^alpha_nu](s_nu A_1...A_j),

where s_nu is an allowed initial row of cost epsilon_nu and

  epsilon_nu + support(alpha_nu) <= L.

Let M_beta be an actual coefficient matrix of the suffix
A_{j+1}...A_e, supported on at most h suffix blocks. Disjointness of the
prefix and suffix variable blocks gives

  r M_beta
    =sum_nu c_nu [X^alpha_nu Y^beta](s_nu A_1...A_e).

Every coefficient on the right has cost at most L+h. This is an exact
coefficient identity, not merely a convolution estimate. Linear
combinations of suffix coefficients obey the same span bound. Changes
of basis and subtraction of reachable rows also preserve the bound,
because a bounded-cost witness space is an E-vector space.

Apply this observation to an inductive stable subphase at active level
b. If its initial active lifts have prefix witnesses of cost at most L
and its passive generators have witnesses of cost at most L+1, the
weighted lemma at level b uses at most G(b) suffix blocks for active
lifts and at most G(b)-1 for passive generators. Both types therefore
have global cost at most L+G(b). If G(b)=0, there is no passive
contribution in that budget; at the base level b=W there are no passive
coordinates at all.

A following growth step multiplies by one fresh variable block and
adds at most one to the cost. A new active basis and a new passive
kernel basis can then be chosen as field-linear combinations within
the cost bound L+G(b)+1. In particular, one may use this as the next
active-lift bound, with passive allowance one larger. This is the
witness justification for the support recurrences in the manuscript.

For a zero-reset source, take a coefficient p of Z_j. Normalization
defining Z_j involves only block j, while B_{j-1} is the constant-prefix
propagation of B_0. Consequently (0,p) has an actual original-prefix
witness of cost at most one. Restart the actual suffix from B_j with
cost 0 and from (0,p) with cost 1. The positive-passive case then uses
at most G(a)-1 suffix blocks for that passive source. Inserting its
one-block prefix witness yields total cost at most G(a). The B_j
contribution has prefix cost zero and uses at most G(a) suffix blocks.

This accounting does not assign a support cost to a reset multiplier.
Its only inputs are the unweighted reset inclusion, the one-block
witness for the source row, and disjointness at the restart cut.

4. THE EXPLICIT RESET CONTROL -L R^{-1}
   Unit-rank graph lemma and zero-passive reset;
   source lines 174-191 and 289-309.

Suppose Q_c...Q_e=0 and write P=A_c...A_e. Its constant matrix is
diag(I_a,0). The interval hypothesis gives unit rank a, so P has an
invertible active a-by-a pivot and vanishing larger minors. The graph
lemma says that a row in the row module of P with zero active part
must be entirely zero.

First assume d=dim T>0. Let H be the independent d-by-a active row
matrix of B_{c-1}, and choose J with HJ=I_d. By active stability, all
coefficient rows of the active part of B_{c-1}P belong to rowspan(H).
There is therefore a unique d-by-d matrix R over the interval ring
such that

  (B_{c-1}P)_active = R H.

It can be obtained by multiplying that active part by J. Its constant
term is I_d, so R is invertible in the finite local interval ring.

For a fixed p in K_{c-1}, active stability likewise gives a unique
1-by-d row L satisfying

  ((0,p)P)_active = L H.

Again L is that active part times J. Set

  a_{c-1}=-L R^{-1}.

Then the active part of

  (a_{c-1} B_{c-1}+(0,p))P

is (-L R^{-1}R+L)H=0. This output belongs to the row module of P, so
the graph lemma makes its entire output zero. Its final active
coordinate a_e is zero as well: a_e H=0 and HJ=I_d.

The control may involve the entire interval. Section 2 above explains
why that does not obstruct its use in the unweighted coefficient-span
argument. The exact evolution identities of the weighted lemma can
therefore be unrolled to give

  0=p Ftilde_c...Ftilde_e
      +sum_{j=c}^e a_j Z_j Ftilde_{j+1}...Ftilde_e.

The term j=e vanishes because a_e=0. Moving the remaining sum to the
other side gives the reset identity in the manuscript.

If d=0, no H, J, R, L, or active control is needed. A reachable row has
zero active output throughout. Its output through P belongs to the row
module of P and has zero active part, so it is zero by the same graph
lemma. The source-free version of the evolution gives the required
reset in that case.

END OF COMPANION

All four explanations use only the manuscript's stated finite monomial
rings, disjoint blocks, field-linear coefficient spans, active stability,
and unit-rank interval hypotheses. They introduce no new hypothesis.
