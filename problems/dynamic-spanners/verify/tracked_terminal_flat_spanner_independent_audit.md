# Independent audit of the tracked-terminal flat-forest spanner

**25 October 2026. Full mathematical audit passed with the explicit
canonical-owner bookkeeping now included in the main note.**

Audited construction: `tracked_terminal_flat_spanner_four_fifths.md`,
Sections 1--9. Audited local data structure:
`dynamic_marked_forest_short_path_union.md`. The retained flat-family
import was checked against `destructive_apsp_path_support_extractor.md`,
its independent audit, and the typed-null metric lift in
`separated_metric_forest_and_strong_sssp.md`, Section 5.

This audit concerns a fully dynamic spanner whose ordinary total
recourse may depend on insertions. It does not transfer the stronger
insertion-independent recourse of the separate five-sixths theorem,
nor improve the exact general-matching exponent.

## 1. The imported family supplies the necessary handles and metric

At each native level the family includes its nearest-pivot forest,
all strict-ball shortest-path trees, and the exact trees needed at an
actual-size base. The application additionally names the exact local
trees for initially small untouched components; their size is bounded
by the same fixed polynomial in k. Both balls and inverse clusters
are bounded, so the local family has polynomial-in-k pointwise
occurrence. A ball-size bound alone would not prove that claim.

Each forest-node occurrence has a distinguished handle in its parent
lift. Distinct occurrences that have the same physical image are not
identified. A child-node claim assigns a pool index; its terminal is
the root of that particular pool tree. Repeating this map gives its
current root-flat handle. Pool nodes keep their physical images, and
generation replacement retires the old version before publishing the
new one. Thus a path query between the two handles represents the
correct input forest path. These are occurrence-local handles, not a
claim that every equal-image auxiliary copy is a canonical terminal.

The native child graph uses positive port scales. A selected edge
retains its parent edge and root-leg witnesses; their literal length
is no greater than its stored label. A port connector or immediate
child-null edge becomes a true-null link at that projection. Hence
every lifted path has parent length at most its child length divided
by the port scale. Repeating the projection gives the cumulative-scale
metric inequality used in the spanner proof.

At the final root, copies of actual root-native edges retain their
root-native lengths. Thus an original graph edge has length H and a
root port connector has length one; null links generated by higher
projections have length zero. The output aggregate distinguishes
original physical edges from both kinds of non-output connectors.
This is consistent with the H-normalized numerical query bound.

## 2. Flat families do not multiply k at every ancestor

The local family is added at its birth level, then lifted through its
ancestors using the existing pool interface. For local occurrence
b_star=k^O(1) polylog(N), its recurrence has the form

    G_j <= b_star + P (G_(j-1)+1).

The polynomial-in-k factor is an additive local contribution, not a
new factor attached to every already lifted family at every level.
The coupled initialization/update recurrence retains the initial
selected allowance that can later become child edits. It yields the
audited full transcript bound (n+m_0+I+D) exp(O(B)), including dormant
pools and retained isolates.

The spanner observer does not alter that hierarchy or cause a recursive
native update. Its own work may multiply the completed transcript by
a fixed polynomial in k and later by the short-path cap R. Those are
single factors in the final work bound, not recursive feedback.

## 3. The terminal inventory starts with n original handles

At the root, S_0 consists only of the n original query handles q_v.
It does not contain all O(n+m) native port vertices. At a later level,
the image of a marked native vertex is the fixed child query handle
q(p_i(v)) of its nearest pivot, when that child handle is exposed.
This is compatible with positive-port resets and does not require
the newer no-reset hierarchy.

The nearest-pivot forest has one designated pivot per component.
Maintain the number of S_i marks in each component. A component with
positive count contributes one Boolean child mark. A membership event
at one native vertex can toggle only that component's one output.
Native forest cuts, links, root changes and exposure changes create
only constantly many additional events. Therefore

    b_(i+1) <= b_i + O(l_i),

where l_i is the full native forest/root/exposure transcript. Summing
over levels costs a depth factor, not k to the depth. A reference
count at a shared output handles coincident aliases without changing
this argument.

An untouched small component may have a private pivot with no child
handle. It contributes no next-level mark. A query there terminates
in its local exact tree, or is disconnected from the other endpoint.
The first actual activation exposes its old pivot and enables the
ordinary propagation. This boundary is essential: a missing handle
cannot be treated as a free recursive vertex.

## 4. Marks and ownership events

In each nearest-pivot forest, mark S_i and their native pivots, at most
2n distinct nodes. For a strict-ball, base or local exact tree, activate
the source precisely when it lies in S_i and mark all native nodes of
that source tree. There are at most n active sources per level, and
each such tree has only k^O(1) polylog(N) nodes. Consequently the total
marked-root inventory is n k^O(1) polylog(N), counted separately in
different forest occurrences.

Marking every node of an active source tree avoids a moving-distance
eligibility problem. A source toggle costs a polynomial in k once.
Native forest-node births and retirements are already explicit; if
their source is active they induce one corresponding mark event.
Forest-edge edits alone do not require rescanning all marks.

One necessary clarification was added during this audit. Releasing
an input node's pool index need not retire the physical pool-root
record: that record may remain as an unused allocated ghost. The
observer therefore listens to canonical-owner claims and releases as
well as node birth/death. A release removes the former owner's mark
and tag before the index can be reused. The same fixed current handle
maps permit this in polynomial logarithmic time. No tag is copied to
all auxiliary pool nodes. These events are part of the actual input
node/ownership transcript and add no new asymptotic term.

## 5. Independent check of dynamic marked-forest compression

The minimal subtree connecting q marked nodes has O(q) compressed
vertices after suppressing unmarked degree-two nodes. A singleton
marked component contributes only its isolated mark. Nonnegative
lengths and null edges do not change this topological count.

The local dynamic proof is valid for general forest updates:

* For a new mark, a path to any existing mark reaches the old marked
  subtree at its projection. The first edge painted with a compressed
  path ID identifies that projection, including the case that the new
  mark is internal to an old compressed path. If the old subtree is a
  singleton, no painted edge exists and the old mark is the projection.
* Removing a leaf mark removes one compressed path and can create only
  one new degree-two branch suppression. Removing an internal mark
  either changes its flag or merges its two incident paths.
* A forest cut inside the marked subtree breaks exactly one compressed
  path. Its two unmarked tails contain no retained branch or mark and
  disappear from the minimal subtree. Only its endpoints can require
  suppression. A cut outside that subtree changes no compressed record.
* A link between two marked components can be implemented by temporary
  endpoint marks, one link, and removal of those temporary marks. When
  either component has no marks, the old nonempty marked subtree is
  unchanged. Temporary marks are private compression metadata, not
  additional terminal traces propagated to the numerical hierarchy.
* An edge-length change belongs to at most one compressed path and
  changes only that record's length and exposure status.

Lazy path-ID assignment, path sums and first-positive-edge search
supply these operations without traversing suppressed paths. Component
mark counts can be maintained by a second dynamic-forest representation
sharing the same edit log. All structural operations take polynomial
logarithmic time. The proof needs no dynamic distance-threshold oracle.

An exposed record stores its physical output list. Retirement uses
that cache while old metadata is available. A long record has no cache
and is not expanded merely because it is cut. A new short path has at
most R physical occurrences, and a separate physical-count aggregate
skips null and root-port connector runs without traversing them. Global
reference counts handle repeated original IDs and different forest
copies conservatively.

## 6. Every required witness is in the selected union

For a living original unit edge uv, its root-native query estimate is
at most 2H gamma_native. Set R to the ceiling of that quantity. Its
query witness is a sum of nonnegative local legs divided by their
correct cumulative port scales. Every constituent root-flat path is
therefore at most R long.

Both endpoints of every constituent are marked. A nearest-pivot leg
starts at a tracked chain vertex and ends at its marked pivot. A
terminal strict-ball, base or local exact leg starts at an active
source, whose whole native tree was marked. No future query is needed
to install these marks.

A path between two marks is a concatenation of whole compressed edges
in their minimal marked subtree. Since lengths are nonnegative, each
such edge is no longer than that path, hence at most R. All of them
are selected. Their projected physical walks concatenate even when
the flat endpoint occurrence IDs differ, because their physical images
agree. The union therefore contains a uv-walk of original length at
most 2 gamma_native. Replacing each edge of an original shortest path
proves the stated all-pairs stretch.

The argument is simultaneous for all original edges. It never scans
and stores one separate witness per input edge after every update.

## 7. Work, size and parameter scope

The compressed candidate count is at most the total mark count, up to
a constant factor. Exposing at most R occurrences per candidate gives
size n k^O(1) R polylog(N). The forest and mark transcript is paid by
(n+m_0+I+D) exp(O(B)); each of its operations changes constantly many
compressed records and costs at most (R+1) polylog(N) to update caches.
Thus the final work is (n+m_0+I+D) exp(O(B+log R)).

Initial graph credit remains in this transcript even when spent after
initialization. It would be unjustified to drop m_0 or I from the safe
ordinary total-recourse bound. Output size, by contrast, is controlled
by the current n-based mark inventory.

For the retained normalized positive-port family,
B=O(U/t+t^3 h), log R=O(t^4 h+h), and log k=Theta(U/t).
Both work and output density have envelope O(U/t+t^4 h). Taking
t=Theta((U/h)^(1/5)) gives the claimed four-fifths point. The shrink
guard has slack, and this substitution uses the already proved
positive-port normalized interface, not an unproved high-degree
normalized no-reset interface.

The finer-stretch point pays the explicit k-factor in its density;
it does not obtain density n exp(O(A_0)) merely because the stretch
has logarithm A_0. The public-provider comparison retains its old
full-copy lift and its own repair cost. No colored-pool work reduction
is credited to that public comparison. Broader novelty and literature
claims remain subject to the separate public-baseline audit.

## 8. Conclusion

No remaining gap was found in the specified tracked-terminal observer,
dynamic compression, or their composition with the retained hierarchy.
This establishes the stated four-fifths dynamic-spanner point as an
internally audited research proof assembly. Its principal remaining
limits are ordinary insertion-dependent recourse, amortized update
time, subpolynomial stretch and density, and dependence on the earlier
research provider imports.
