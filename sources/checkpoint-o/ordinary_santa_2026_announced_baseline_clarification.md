# Ordinary Santa Claus: a 2026 announced algorithm and baseline precision

**Primary-source check performed 25 October 2026.**

This note records a newly located public announcement relevant to
ordinary restricted Santa Claus. It updates comparison wording without
treating a seminar abstract as a fully checked algorithm theorem.
The recipient-polymatroid results in this project remain a different
model and do not acquire a new comparator merely from this announcement.

## 1. What was located

| Source | Exact status relevant to this audit |
|---|---|
| [Cheng and Mao, arXiv:1905.06084v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06084), submitted 15 May 2019 | An independently available constructive `(4+delta)` approximation for ordinary restricted max-min allocation, relative to the configuration LP, with polynomial time for fixed positive delta. Its later Algorithmica version is DOI `10.1007/s00453-022-00942-y`. |
| [Haxell and Szabo, arXiv:2202.01143v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.01143v3), version history dated 27 January 2025 | Theorem 1.1 proves configuration-LP integrality gap at most `53/15`, commonly rounded upward to 3.534. The end of Section 1.1 explicitly separates this from a controlled running time for finding an allocation. |
| [Kumar and Narayanaswamy, Theory of Computing Systems 69, article 38](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00224-025-10241-y), published 11 November 2025 | The publisher's abstract states a polynomial `(12+epsilon)` algorithm using BS-clustering and geometric programming. This published abstract does not itself state a constructive 3.534 bound. |
| [IIT Madras official departmental thesis-defense abstract](https://cse.iitm.ac.in/spotlight.php?highlight=140), Anil Kumar, 29 January 2026 | The abstract announces a polynomial `(3.534+epsilon)` approximation for ordinary restricted Santa Claus. The university's [theory-group notice](https://theory.cse.iitm.ac.in/viewevent.php?id=87&type=news) independently confirms the thesis defense and title. No complete proof of the announced algorithm was located in this bounded pass. |

## 2. What the bounded follow-up established

The follow-up checked the exact thesis title, author and numerical
bound through both search systems, the official theory-group research
listing, the author's official group profile, the publisher page for
the geometric-programming paper, and the current primary Haxell--Szabo
version. These checks did not locate a complete thesis manuscript or
an accessible full theorem proving the announced polynomial 3.534
construction.

This is an access and verification limit, not evidence that the
announcement is false or that no manuscript exists. A thesis can
contain results newer than its author's published articles. Conversely,
the older 12+epsilon article cannot by itself verify a stronger result
announced later. A complete theorem, assumptions and running-time
argument are still needed before promoting the new announcement to a
checked constructive comparator.

The coincidence with the known 53/15 integrality gap also does not
settle that question. An existential or estimation bound and a
polynomial algorithm constructing an allocation are distinct claims.
The Haxell--Szabo source itself keeps this distinction explicit.

## 3. Wording to use in this checkpoint

The safe positive statement is:

> Cheng and Mao independently provide a polynomial `(4+delta)`
> construction for ordinary restricted Santa Claus. A January 2026
> university thesis abstract announces `(3.534+epsilon)`; its full
> algorithm proof has not been checked in this audit.

Here the blockquote is our proposed wording, not a quotation of any
source. Avoid an unqualified assertion that four is the definitive
current best ordinary-model approximation. Also avoid treating the
3.534 announcement as a fully verified replacement before its proof
is available for examination.

The previous DRZ version correction remains a separate issue. Its
current proof must be cited at its current stated guarantee, while
Cheng--Mao's independent constructive result remains available.
Neither event invalidates unrelated proofs or model-specific results.

## 4. Why this does not change the full-polymatroid comparison

The announcement concerns explicit indivisible gifts, each giving a
common positive value to a subset of children and zero to the others.
It does not state arbitrary recipient-polymatroid supply constraints,
binary multiplicities, optional player-basis selection, or the new
corank parameters used here. An extension to those models would need
its own theorem and proof.

Accordingly, the already checked Bamas et al. recipient-polymatroid
guarantees remain the directly stated public comparator for that
model. Our near-two parameterized algorithms and the unrelated-value
additive theorems also retain their exact scope and additive losses.
No paper in this note is being claimed universally dominated by them.

## 5. Additional parameterized-source context

A separate current primary item,
[Kumar and Narayanaswamy, CALDAM 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-17156-6_22),
published 13 February 2026, states parameterized hardness for combinations
of target value and incidence-graph structural parameters. The public
abstract describes treewidth, clique-width and diameter combinations;
it does not state a corank algorithm or an obstruction at our particular
parameters. We do not infer either from the title alone.

For broader historical context, the primary
[Dagstuhl Seminar 23291 report, Section 5.3](https://doi.org/10.4230/DagRep.13.7.96)
records that ordinary unrelated Santa Claus already has a parameterized
approximation scheme with the number of children as parameter, citing
Jansen and Maack. It asks for other useful parameterizations. This
reinforces the need to compare the actual parameter and representation,
rather than label every parameterized allocation approximation as new.

This update is a bounded source audit. It makes no exhaustive novelty
claim and does not alter the mathematical status of the internally
reviewed project theorems.
